## The review

**Setup.** The worktree began at an older commit and was reset to d94d911.
Throwaway probes stayed uncommitted in the worktree.

**What checks out.** All twenty worked examples pass. A build-biased
real-play sweep over 55,976 positions in Classic, Royal and Royal-14 found
`legal_moves` equal to the reference every time, and `candidate_moves` empty
exactly when `legal_moves` was. Scoring and ties, the last-card sweep and the
residue, the residue to the last capturer or nobody, scoring only at the end
of a hand, the cut, the deal order, control passing on a raise or add, and
the single/multiple flags all match RULES.md. 200k random strings through
`Move::parse` and random commands through `Session::send` raised no panic.
No information leak in `View`, the agents, `advice::notes` or the session
events.

### F1. One ace "guards" a 1-build and a 14-build at once (critical, confirmed)

Royal with aces 1 or 14. South holds AS AC 5D 7H; the table AD 9S 2C 3C.
South builds 1 (AS on AD, holding AC); North trails; South builds 14 (5D on
9S): legal, since the one AC counts as holding 14 and still as holding 1.
North trails. South now holds 7H AC against `QH KH 2C 3C [1* @S: AS AD]
[14 @S: 9S 5D]` and has no legal move: the ace as 1 leaves the 14-build
unguarded, as 14 the 1-build; the 7 neither captures nor builds; a
controller may not trail. Every agent then panics (greedy indexes
`moves[0]`, legal calls `below(0)`, the counter and searcher expect a
candidate). In a session, a stuck North panics `advance()` (in WebAssembly,
a trap, and the deterministic replay traps again); a stuck South is
soft-locked. 19 of 20,000 random Royal-14 hands got stuck, and a person can
make the position on purpose. Rule 7 read build by build is satisfied, but
its stated consequences (a legal move always exists; never more controlled
values than cards) fail. Fix: the distinct values a player controls need
distinct held cards. Only the ace has two values, so for 1–13 a card of that
rank each, and for 14, `1 + [controls a 1-build]` aces. Mirror it
independently in the reference and the sampler.

### F2. The partition search is exponential (major, confirmed)

Royal, 29 loose cards (2–7♠, Q♠, 2–8 of hearts, diamonds and clubs, Q♥):
`take KS` and all 29 total 182 = 14 × 13 but cannot split into thirteens, and
`check` takes 9.3 s to say so (22 cards: 148 ms). Reachable from
`Session::send` and from `offer()` as cards are tapped. Splitting depends
only on the multiset of values: memoize on the count vector, with early
rejections.

### F3. Hint, verdict and solver blow up on busy tables (major, confirmed)

`advice::assessed` evaluates every candidate in 32 worlds. Deal 4, South
holding 9C TD 8S 7H: 8 loose cards (129 candidates), hint 1.6 s; 12 loose
(456), 39.8 s; 16 loose (552), over four minutes. The counter and searcher
take 23–1,200 ms on the same tables. A last deal with 16 loose cards: the
solver takes 18 s (2.8M nodes). Ordinary play is fast (hints average 4 ms),
and busy tables are rare (0.33% of decisions at level 1). Fix: shortlist plus
the person's move; budgets with a fallback; run the engine in a Web Worker.

### F4. The selection interface cannot offer some legal moves (minor, confirmed)

Classic, table A–6 of every suit, South TD 9C: `take TD AS 2S 3S 4S 6S AH
3H` passes `check`, but `offer` for exactly that selection returns no moves,
because it filters the bounded candidates. Decide the exact selection with
`check`.

### F5. Replay loses the aids; `RECORD_VERSION` is unused (minor, confirmed)

`set` commands never enter the record, so an explain-on sitting replays
without its notes or verdicts. `RECORD_VERSION` is never referenced, so an
old record replays silently into a different game. Replay accepts `*`
entries without checking the move was the only one.

### F6. Panics reachable through the public API (minor)

Confirmed: a NaN skill (level 0 at `Opponent::new`); `words::describe` with a
value above 14. By reading: `rate` with an illegal move, `hint` out of turn,
`apply` with an illegal move (or a silently corrupted table).

### F7. Watch mode steps two moves at a time (minor, confirmed)

`step()` plays South's move and North's reply in one call.

### F8. Terminal `--watch --explain` reveals South's hand (minor, confirmed at d94d911)

The terminal client computed notes from South's view and worded them as
"You", printing "You can sweep the table with your queen." while South's
hand was hidden.

### Test gaps

No test of: the matching obligation; any bound on the cost of `check`, hint,
`rate` or the solver; `offer` completeness; replay with aids, or record
versions; settings validation; one move per watch step; a build of 1; a
hand-level last-card sweep with an empty residue; an agent leak test.
`random_positions_vary` asserts coverage for Classic only. The oracle shares
`holds_value` and the build-by-build obligation with the code under test,
which is why F1 slipped through. Tests that cannot fail, or nearly:
`the_leak_test_catches_a_planted_leak` plants no leak in `View`; the
residue test's sweep assertion is true by construction; the table-items
trail test never checks a build and can skip its trail check;
`a_position_that_keeps_the_obligation_always_has_a_move` shares the flawed
predicate.

### Checklist vs RULES.md

Adding to a multiple build at its value is legal (5c); only raising one is
forbidden: keep RULES.md. A controller may capture other cards while still
holding another card of the build's value (rule 7, W13): keep RULES.md. A
last-card capture that takes everything is a sweep, while the residue never
is (rules 8 and 9, after pagat): the user's call, and a setting would be one
line. Royal aces count 1 in builds, a recorded interpretation: keep it; but
the checklist's worry is vindicated by F1, where one ace counted as 1 and
14 at once. The other checklist items agree with RULES.md and the code.

### Design notes for the protocol

A panic-free boundary (fix F1 and F6; agents returning `Option`). Budgets,
a Web Worker, and the client driving one step at a time. Canonical moves
(`onto` may name any card in a build). Machine-readable refusals. Expose
state only from `view()`. A u64 seed as a JSON number loses precision above
2^53. Normalize `Rules` (aces at 14 off in Classic), or identical games hash
differently. `Events` has a fixed capacity of 8 with an unchecked push.
Doc drift: DESIGN 11.2 (the solver plays for the margin, not the game) and
§8 (a `Left` event that is only a note).

### Overall

The rules core is strong: the move generator and `check` agree with an
independent brute-force reading of RULES.md on tens of thousands of
positions and get every contested point right. The serious problem is one
rules-level hole (F1), which needs a sentence in RULES.md and a matching
obligation in the code, the oracle and the sampler. The rest is robustness:
the exponential partition search, unbounded hint and solver cost on busy
tables, a few panicking public functions, and replay without versions.
