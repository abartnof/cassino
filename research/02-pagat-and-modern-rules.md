## A. Pagat.com — standard Anglo-American Casino [S1]

### A.1 Identity, name, history claims (pagat)
- Casino "is the only fishing game to have become popular in English speaking countries." [S1]
- Origin: "Although it is traditionally supposed to have originated in Italy, there is no direct evidence of it having been played there, at least under that name." [S1]
- "Casino first appears in the card game literature at the end of the eighteenth century in London, and shortly afterwards in Germany. In the late nineteenth century it became fashionable in America and a number of new variations were developed." [S1]
- Spelling: "the earliest sources use the spelling Casino, but a tradition has grown up among later writers to spell it with a double 's': Cassino." [S1]
- Pagat's history note "draws on an article by Franco Pratesi: Casino from Nowhere to Vaguely Everywhere (originally published in The Playing-Card Vol XXIV No 1 July/Aug 1995 pages 6-11)"; page partly based on contributions from Kim Scarborough, Janet Levy, Scott Grengs and Rachel Hooley. [S1]
- The page is © John McLeod 1996–2026, "Last updated: 6th May 2026". [S1]
- Pagat's fishing-games index defines the family: "each player has a hand of cards and there is a layout of face up cards on the table... If it matches a card or cards in the layout, the played card and the matched cards are captured". In "the Western style of fishing games, cards are only played from the hand, not turned up from the stock" (vs. Oriental/hanafuda games). [S2]

### A.2 Players, cards, deal [S1]
- 2–4 players ("though in theory more could take part"); "one of the few games which will deal out evenly to two, three, or four players". Four can play two-vs-two partnerships, partners opposite. [S1]
- 52-card deck; numeral cards ace–ten count face value (ace=1). In the standard pagat version "picture cards, not having a numerical value, can only capture an equal picture." [S1]
- Deal: four cards each and four face up in the centre ("laid out separately so that all are visible"). "Traditionally, the deal is in twos: two cards to each other player, two to the table, then two to the dealer, then repeat. However, some players prefer to deal the cards singly." [S1]
- Re-deals of four each, no further table cards: "6 deals for 2 players, 4 deals for three players, 3 deals for 4 players". [S1]
- **Spoken call**: "The dealer must announce "last" when dealing the last cards." Deal passes left; play clockwise starting left of dealer. [S1]

### A.3 Types of play [S1]
1. **Capturing with a face card**: K/Q/J captures only a matching face card; "If the table contains more than one matching card only one may be captured." Example: two queens on table, a queen played captures one, "but not both". [S1]
2. **Capturing with a numeral card**: captures same-rank cards and sets summing to its rank; each captured card can only belong to one set. Example: with A 2 3 5 6 8 on table, an eight "could capture 8 6 2 5 3 or 8 5 2 A, but not all six cards." [S1]
3. **Building**: "The player making the build must announce the capturing number (saying, for example, "building 5"), and must hold a numeral card which can later make the capture." Two types: **single builds** ("a 5-build made of a 2 and a 3") and **multiple builds** ("a 9-build made of 6-3 plus 5-4 plus 9"). "When you make a build, it must include the card you just played - you cannot create a build consisting entirely of cards that were already on the table." [S1]
   - Example (verbatim call): a player holding 3 and 8 with a 5 on table "may put the 3 on the 5 and announce "building 8"". [S1]
   - With A and 2 on table and holding two 3s and a 6, playing a 3 can: capture A+2; make a single build announcing "building 6"; or a multiple build announcing "building 3". Illegal to build 4 or 5 since not held. [S1]
   - Table 3,4,5,7; holding 2 and 7: play the 2 to make a five-card multiple 7-build (5+2, 3+4, 7). [S1]
4. **Capturing a build**: only by a numeral card of the announced rank; "It is thus possible to "steal" a build created by another player". [S1]
   - **Owner obligation**: if the table holds a build "which you created or added to yourself, and no other player has added to it since your last turn, you are not allowed simply to trail". You must capture, build, or add to a build. [S1]
   - "you are not allowed to play so as to leave yourself with no card equal to the value of this build." [S1]
   - While capturing a build you may also take loose cards summing to the same number (build of 9 + loose 5 + opponent's 4 → take all). [S1]
   - A 7-build plus a loose 2 cannot be taken by a 9. Two 5s: player must announce "building 10" (single) or "building 5" (multiple); the announced number fixes which rank can capture. [S1]
5. **Adding to a build**: (a) raise a *single* build with a hand card if you hold the new capturing number; may simultaneously incorporate table cards equal to the new number (it then becomes multiple). "You can never use a card from the table to change the value of a single build." (b) Add to any build (single or multiple) a hand card that, alone or with loose table cards, equals the existing number. [S1]
   - "The capturing number of a multiple build can never be changed." [S1]
   - Example A: two 3s announced as 6; holding 2 and 8 → add 2, "building 8"; next player with A and 9 adds A, "building 9". [S1]
   - Example B: A, 2, 4 on table, A+4 built to 5; holding 3, 8, 10 you may play 3 "building eight" but may not incorporate the loose 2 to make 10. [S1]
   - Example C: 3+4 built 7 and a separate 9; holding 2 and 9 → play 2 to make 9 and absorb the loose 9: "saying "building nine"". [S1]
   - "when adding to a build you are never allowed to break up or rearrange the cards that are already in the build. In particular the cards in a single build are irrevocably joined". [S1]
6. **Trailing**: "Playing a card without building or capturing is sometimes called trailing. You are allowed to trail a card even if that card could have made a capture." Partnership example: trail your 10 next to partner's 10 to leave both for partner. "The only case in which trailing is not allowed is when there is a build on the table which you were the last player to add to." [S1]
- **Last cards / residue**: "the last player who made a capture also wins any cards which are left on the table (these cards are sometimes known as the residue). That is why it is important that the dealer should announce "last"". [S1]
- **Strategy hint**: "it is often good for the dealer to hold back a face card to play last if possible; this will capture a matching face card on the table and thereby also win any other table cards that remain." [S1]

### A.4 Scoring (pagat standard) [S1]
- Eleven points per hand: Most Cards 3; Most Spades 1; each Ace 1; ten of diamonds "called Big Casino or the Good Ten" 2; two of spades "called Little Casino or the Good Two" 1. [S1]
- Slang: "capturing an ace with another ace is called "cash" by some players". [S1]
- Ties for cards/spades: no one scores. Game to 21 over as many rounds as needed; if two reach it, higher score wins; tie → another round. [S1]

### A.5 Pagat's listed variations [S1]
- **Building permissiveness** — "many of the card game books are ambiguous on this point":
  - Trail a card and simultaneously build from table cards not including the played card. [S1]
  - Owner of a build may simply trail on later turns. [S1]
  - Table cards may help *increase* a single build's value provided a hand card is also used (Example B becomes legal: 3+2 onto 5-build = 10); but the hand card must contribute to the change of value. [S1]
  - "Some players treat a single build exactly like a single card of the same value" — e.g. 6-build + loose 4 taken by a 10, or combined into a multiple 10-build. [S1]
  - Partnership "build for partner": "building 9 for partner". [S1]
- **Sweeps**: "Many people play that a Sweep is worth one point... Some players call this a clear." Capturing card stored face-up in the won pile to count sweeps. Final-card sweep counts only if it genuinely captures everything; taking residue by right of last capture is not a sweep. [S1]
- **Targets**: play to 11 (two-player/two-team: winner of the round scores the *difference*); play to 50. [S1]
- **Alternative cards point**: 2 for most cards + 1 for last capture. [S1]
- **Connecticut/New York reports**: 3 for 10♦, 2 for 2♠, 1 for most cards. [S1]
- **Belgian/Flemish "Wippen"**: 2 most cards, 2 most spades; "A sweep is known as a Wip and scores one point." [S1]
- **Counting-out order** when several reach the target: cards, spades, big casino, little casino, aces (spades, clubs, hearts, diamonds), sweeps; then another hand. [S1]
- **Claiming / counting out during play**: points counted as earned ("spades or cards as soon as one player has captured 7 or 27 of them respectively"); play ends when a player "correctly claims to have won... (even if the opponent has in fact scored more but failed to claim it)". [S1]
- **Spade Casino**: each spade 1 point, J♠ an extra point, A♠ and 2♠ still extra; total 24 excluding sweeps: "cards (3) + ten of diamonds (2) + the four aces and the two and jack of spades (6) + the spades (13)". [S1]
- German version of pagat page renders the call as „"8er-Build" ansagen“ and the dealer's warning as "Letzte Runde" ansagen. [S29]

## B. Pagat.com — Casino variants and the wider fishing family

### B.1 Royal Casino (pagat) [S3]
- "Royal Casino is the usual English name for versions of Casino in which the picture cards have a numerical value: Jacks 11, Queens 12, Kings 13 and Aces 1 or 14." [S3]
- **North American Royal Casino** (English/American books): differs from ordinary Casino only in values; "an Ace can capture a King and an Ace, counting the capturing Ace as 14 and the captured Ace as 1." Clockwise; captures not compulsory; build owner may not trail; "often played without a score for sweeps. If sweeps are scored... there is no cancelling"; no near-21 restrictions; tie-break counting order: Most cards, Most spades, 10♦, 2♠, aces in order Spades, Clubs, Hearts, Diamonds, Sweeps. [S3]
- **Dominican Republic Casino** (from Humbernio Lockward): 2 players usual, 3–4 possible; counter-clockwise; aces 1 or 14 ("An Ace can capture an 8, 5, and A on the layout. 8 + 5 + 1 = 14"). [S3]
  - Spanish terms: Capture "Captura" or "Cojer"; Build "Forma"; Sweeps "Virados"; Most Spades "espadas"; 10♦ "diez de casino"; 2♠ "dos de casino". [S3]
  - "The owner of a build is the player who most recently added a card to it." No building for partner; owner may not trail. [S3]
  - **Forced capture of matching loose cards**: "It is not possible to trail when playing a card that has a capture value equal to any loose card on the table"; you must capture all matching loose cards or use the card to build — but sums are optional. Example: initial 10,10,8,6, first player plays 10 → "both tens must be captured, even though the next player may be able to score a sweep with an Ace." [S3]
  - Table-talk custom: cards left that could have been taken "are known as "dejado" (left behind) or "pisado" (stepped on). It is customary for the opponents to point out such cards". [S3]
  - You may raise an opponent's single build but "you are not allowed to increase the value of your own build" (partner's is OK). [S3]
  - **Sweep marking & cancellation**: sweep card placed "face under the player or team's capture pile, at right angles... partly visible". "Sweeps of opposing players or teams cancel each other." [S3]
  - Scoring 3/1/2/1/1 + uncancelled sweeps, to 21, with **endgame restrictions**: at 18 can only win by most cards; at 19 only by 10♦; at 20 only by most spades; meeting the criterion wins "automatically, irrespective of how many points the opponent(s) score". Players at 18+ cannot score sweeps but can cancel. [S3]
  - Variants: building for partner when provable, announced "for partner"; Joel A. Soto's version with restrictions from 16 (16: only cards or 10♦; 17: only cards or spades; 18: cards; 19: 10♦; 20: spades). [S3]
- **Haiti**: "pronounced with the stress on the first syllable: cásino"; best for four; J11 Q12 K13, A 1/14; no special scoring mentioned (source: video by Andrew Saint-Remy). [S3]
- **Tuxedo**: American variant with 40 Rook cards (1–10 in four colours), 2–6 players; scoring most cards 15, each "orange" 10, sweep of 4+ cards 10, sweep of ≤3 cards 5, each Five 5; to 100. **Call**: "The "orange" must be called by the player in order to score. If the player forgets, another player can score 10 points by calling "orange". A false call of "orange" incurs a 10 point penalty." An orange = capturing a red and a yellow card at once. HOYLE Card Games (Windows/Mac) includes a Tuxedo program. [S3]
- **Hungarian Casino (Kaszinó)** (David Kirchner; Ulmann 1892 "Casino en deux"; Nagy Kártyakönyv 1990): 2 players, 3 cards per deal, no building; **may play several cards from hand together** to capture equal totals (J+Q+A = 24 takes 4+7+K). First to 11, claiming by correct announcement. Ulmann: first-play sweep of the initial four scores 2; non-dealer may peek at the cut card. Hungarian books: combined value ≤13; after a sweep (tábla) the opponent plays twice in a row; sweeps recorded by a line on the slate; last capture is "csere"; last round played with exposed cards; **call**: a player at 11 "can stop the game and win by saying Ausz!, Ausz vagyok! or Kint vagyok!"; reaching 11 in the first deal = double game; majority of spades "(pikkje van)". [S3]

### B.2 African Casino (Eswatini, Lesotho, South Africa; Namibia note) [S4]
- Characteristic: "captured cards are kept face up, and that a played card can capture not only cards from the central layout but also the top cards of opponents capture pile" (via augmenting builds). Similar game in Windhoek, Namibia: "Omulongo Womadi" (Oshiwambo). [S4]
- **Swazi Casino** (Jonathan Dushoff): 2–4, anticlockwise; J11 Q12 K13 A1/14; first player cuts four cards from the middle for the layout; dealer deals all remaining cards. Suggested re-cut if layout has ≥3 points of scoring cards or ≥3 pictures. [S4]
  - Builds: "single build" and "augmented build"; each build has an owner. A turn may contain several actions; only one hand card played. Single builds can be formed *from table cards only* if you hold the capturing card. [S4]
  - Capture term: "The capture or "chow"". Opponent can insist you take all you're entitled to. "it is legal to play a card that could have captured, but to use it in a build instead... This is called drifting." [S4]
  - Changing the value of a single build only if owned by an opponent; augmented builds' value never changes; may use the top card of opponents' capture pile to augment. "There cannot be two builds of the same capture value on the table at the same time." Never break up builds. [S4]
  - Aces in builds fixed once incorporated; two aces must be announced as augmented 1, single 2, or augmented 14. [S4]
  - Partnership: may build for partner only when partner's earlier build of that rank was changed/taken; may "ask your partner not to capture the build". [S4]
  - Scoring (11 per deal): most cards 2 (tie 1 each); most spades 2 (tie 1 each); 2♠ "spy two" 1; 10♦ "big ten" 2; aces 1 each. "no extra score for sweeps". [S4]
  - Two-player: 12 cards each, twice; "crazy casino" with 8-card hands and draw pile. [S4]
  - **Tactics**: monopoly of aces → build 14s nobody else can touch; high-value build piles are most powerful; capturing early can be a disadvantage because your capture pile becomes available; "Drifting can be used as a way of signalling to your partner" (owner's drift signals two more of the rank; partner's drift signals one). [S4]
- **Sotho Casino**: pictures lose numeric value, ace only 1; partners keep separate piles; may steal partner's top card; Lesotho players observed using 40-card pack. [S4]
- **South African Casino** (Faizal Asmal): 40 cards (pictures removed); "In South Africa, playing a card without capturing is called drifting"; captures stacked lowest on top; top card of each capture pile capturable; build owner may not drift (except second phase of 2-player game); scoring most cards 2, "at least five spades" 1, 2♠ 1, 10♦ 2, aces 1 each. [S4]

### B.3 Nordic Casino (Sweden, Finland) and Mulle [S5]
- Framed as fishing: "the table is seen as an ocean containing a number of face up cards representing fish." Nordic Casino = Royal-type values, "usually played without building". [S5]
- **Swedish Kasino**: 2–4; J11 Q12 K13 A1/14; dealt in pairs, 4 each + 4 table; "Captures are not compulsory." Sweep = **tabbe** (pl. tabbar), recorded by turning the capturing card face up rotated in the pile. [S5]
- **Spoken call**: "When dealing the last cards to the players... the dealer should warn everyone by saying "sistan" (last) or "båt" (boat)." [S5]
- Scoring: most spades 2; most cards 1; each ace 1; 10♦ "known as storan - the big one" 2; 2♠ "known as lillan - the small one" 1; last capture ("sistan") 1; each tabbe 1 → 11 + tabbar; ties not awarded. Usually first to 16; tie → most spades. [S5]
- Tactics: don't leave scoring cards on table; don't trail spades; trail a rank already played; hold a non-spade 2 to catch lillan, non-diamond 10 to catch storan; deliberately leave cards to deny a tabbe. [S5]
- Variant **Överspader**: 1 point per spade over six; played to 21. Targets 11/21/50. [S5]
- **Mulle**: double deck (104); "mulle" = capturing the identical card (same rank and suit) alone; 5 points each (Sassersson); Dan Glimne's version has 10♦ = 16 in hand, 2♠ = 15 in hand, aces 14 in hand / 1 on table, and building as on the Casino page. [S5]
- **Finnish Kasino**: A = 1 on table, 14 from hand ("an Ace cannot capture a single Ace"); 2♠ ("Small Kasino") 2 on table / 15 in hand; 10♦ ("Big Kasino") 10 on table / 16 in hand. Scores: cards 1, spades 2, aces 1, small 1, big 2 = 10; sweep = **mökki** ("hut"), sweeps cancel; no sweeps after the final deal or once a player has 10+; to 16, aces and kasinos counted immediately (mid-hand win possible). [S5]

### B.4 Krypkasino (Swedish reverse Casino) [S6]
- Aim reversed: avoid points; "Playing a card which does not capture anything is known as krypa (creeping or crawling)". Double deck; 2–6 players. 10♦ storan 3, 2♠ lillan 2, other aces/spades 1 (A♠ 2); total 42. Pictures on the table are set aside after each deal for the last capturer. Captures compulsory; forced sweep = **tvångstabbe**; sweep +5 to taker, −5 to the player who set it up; **dubbeltabbe** (identical-card capture of a lone card) ±10. [S6]
- **Spoken call**: "It is customary for a player who sets up a sweep to announce it, saying for example 'sweep for a nine' (tabbe på 9)"; e.g. "'sweep for an ace'", "'sweep for a six or a queen'". Last-deal warning "last cards" ("sistan" / "båt"). [S6]

### B.5 Zwicker (Schleswig-Holstein) [S8]
- "The "Zwick" (equivalent to the English "tweak") is a play by which you capture all the cards from the table". Usually 4 players in partnerships, 52 + 3 jokers (15/20/25); A=1/11, J=2/12, Q=3/13, K=4/14. Initial table cards "sometimes called the the Picture (das Bild)". [S8]
- **Building by subtraction as well as addition**; announce the value as you play ("13" or "3" for a queen); builds can be modified by anyone; "13 for partner"; separate table cards can never be combined into one build. [S8]
- "in serious play your partner is not allowed to point out capture possibilities which you may have overlooked". [S8]
- Scoring: jokers 7/6/5, 10♦ 3, 10♠ 1, 2♠ 1, aces 1, majority 3 (=30) + 1 per Zwick. 6-joker version (Großenwiehe/Sillerup): partner may be asked ("Magger") whether they hold a value; 2♠ called "Pingel". Older jokerless Zwicker (Hülsemann 1930): 10♦ "Cardinal" 10 points, Zwick 3 points. [S8]
- Pagat notes Parlett's *Penguin Book of Card Games* (2000) Zwicker description was based on Claus Grupp's (probably invented) wild-joker version. [S8]
- Tactics: avoid leaving totals of 10/15/20/25 if an opponent may hold the matching high card; avoid playing A/K/Q/J loose (dual values); cards 5–9 give fewest chances. [S8]

### B.6 Diloti (Greek, "declaring" ≈ building) [S9]
- Name from «δηλώνω» "to declare"; combines Kontsina and Xeri. 2 players (6-card hands) or 4 in partnership. [S9]
- **Plain declaration** (≈ single build): "Usually the player will verbally declare the value of the pile, for example playing an Ace, combining it with a loose 2 and 5 and declaring "eight", though this is not obligatory." May be **"raised"** only by the opponent (not by yourself). [S9]
- **Group/family declaration** (≈ multiple build): announced "by saying "two 9's" or "group of 9's" or "family of 9's"; this is not normally obligatory, but must be done if there is ambiguity". [S9]
- Obligations: declarer must capture before laying cards or new declarations. **Xeri** («ξερή» "dry") = clearing the table, 10 points; first card of a round doesn't count. Scoring: cards 4, aces 1 each, "the good 10" (10♦) 2, "the good 2" (2♣) 1, xeri 10 each; to 61 (or 121). [S9]
- Variants: forced capture; plain declarations treated as single cards (cf. Casino permissive variant); "soy" = group declaration or pair of equal cards. [S9]
- Kontsina: "the good 2" / "the good 10" are literal translations of «το 2 το καλό» / «το 10 το καλό» ("lucky 2/lucky 10"). [S21]

### B.7 Other fishing games on pagat (key points relevant to Casino design)
- **Scopa** [S10]: 40 cards; K10 Q/Horse9 J8; 3-card hands, 4 to table; redeal if 3+ kings in layout; capture is compulsory if the played card can capture; single match takes priority over sum; sweep (*scopa*) recorded face-up sideways; last-card capture is never a scopa; points: scope, cards, coins, settebello, primiera (7=21, 6=18, A=16, 5=15, 4=14, 3=13, 2=12, pictures 10); to 11. "chiamarsi fuori" = claiming out mid-hand. Variants: Asso Pigliatutto, Scopa d'Assi, Scopa di Quindici, Re Bello, Napola, Scalette, Bazzica, Corsican (capture not compulsory). [S10]
- **Chkobba** (Tunisia/France): 40 cards, Q/J values reversed; capture compulsory; terms "Kārṭa", "Dīnārī", "Barmīla", "Sabʿa l-ḥayya"; to 21. **Hurrikan** (German 1930s): sweep called "Hurrikan", hearts bonus suit, target 16. [S10]
- **Scopone** [S11]: 9-card hands; strategy: leave ≥11 on table to block sweeps; "whirlwind" sequences of sweeps; establish an "anchor"; track paired/unpaired cards — the dealer's last card. 10-card "Scientific" Scopone; Chitarrella's rules (1937, attributed). [S11]
- **Escoba** [S12]: "broom"; capture sets totalling 15 with the played card; Sota labelled 10 but worth 8 etc.; initial layout summing to 15 → dealer's escoba; strategy: card values total 220 → "at the end of the play there will be 10, 25, 40 or 55 points left on the table". [S12]
- **Cuarenta** (Ecuador) [S13]: "The play is supposed to be full of bravado, loud, exciting, even silly." Calls: **"Ronda"** for three of a kind dealt; capture of the card just played = **caída**; clearing = **limpia**; "caída y limpia". **Score kept with the removed 8s, 9s and 10s**: face-up card = 2 points, face-down = 10 ("perro"). Customs: "snapped" caída flourish; holding a card face down to "predict" a caída; card on forehead taunt. [S13]
- **Pâsur** (Iran) [S14]: capture by totalling 11; jacks sweep but don't score a Sur; Sur cancels opponent's Sur; scoring: 7+ clubs ("Haft Khâj") 7, aces 1, jacks 1, 2♣ 2, 10♦ 3, Sur 5; to 62; stop the game by saying **"per shodam"**. Tactic: leave ≥11 on table; clear with a jack before an opponent must play to an empty table. [S14]
- **Basra** (Egypt/Middle East) [S15]: table called the "floor"; jack takes all (no bonus); 7♦ semi-wild; basra = 10 points; majority 30 points (carried over on a tie); to 101; loser pays for "shisa and beverages". Lebanese (Khuri): jack called **"ashush"**, "imam that 'eats' everything"; cards "eat" cards. [S15]
- **Xeri** (Greek; single-pile like Pişti) [S16]; **Tablić / Tabinet** (Balkans/Romania) [S17]: K14 Q13 J12 A1/11; capture not obligatory; sweep **"tabla"** (Macedonia: "peeshee"); 10♦ 2, other 10s 1, 2♣ 1, A/K/Q/J 1 each, most cards 3 → 25; to 101; **claiming victory** mid-hand; last table cards always go to the dealer's team. [S17]
- **Seep/Sweep** (N. India/Pakistan) [S20]: builds called "houses" ("cemented" houses); bidding for a house from first 4 cards; game ends with a 100-point lead ("baazi"). [S20]
- **Cirulla** (Liguria) [S22]: "lively, noisy"; contrast with Scopone "requiring silence, concentration" → "anti-Scopone"; claiming a hand bonus by **knocking on the table (bussare)**. [S22]
- **Cau Robat** (Catalonia) [S23]: calls **"cau"** ("pronounced "cow""), **"recau"**, **"contracau"** for successive matches of a just-played card. [S23]
- **Ronda** (Morocco) [S24]: calls "ronda"/"tringa" on dealt pairs/triplets; capturing the just-played card: player "calls "one""; next player "five"; with table clear "two", "six", "eleven". [S24]
- **Porrazo** (SW USA, from Mexico): calls "ronda"/"rondine" (US: "rondo", "randa", "randine"); "limpia" → "olympia"; dealer's "es tendido" → "estanditto". [S25]
- **Eléwénjewé** (Yoruba, Nigeria): capture = "scooping"/"scoping"; building = **"Esun"**; joker take-all "Ipalemo". [S26]
- **Žandari** (Bosnia): a jack played to an empty table is mocked — "your Jack has dried up". [S27]
- **Stealing Bundles / Steal the Old Man's Bundle / Casita Robada / Rubamazzo**: children's game; matching the top card of an opponent's bundle steals the whole bundle; largest bundle wins. [S7]
- **Snitch'ems** (Sheffield; *Covent Garden Magazine* Oct 1773; *Sporting Magazine* Dec 1797): capture by pairing, "making eights and tens", and pairing 8s/10s; compulsory capture; only majority of cards scores — Pratesi calls majority-only "the distinctive character of ancient games of [the fishing] family". Addy (1888) and Wright (1904) both "liken the game to Casino". [S18]
- **Laugh and Lie Down**: "A rather unusual fishing game for five players, played in England in the 16th and 17th centuries"; pagat links to Parlett's reconstruction. [S19]

### B.8 Franco Pratesi, "Casino from Nowhere, to Vaguely Everywhere" (PDF linked from pagat) [S30]
- Name: the double-s spelling "is said to be a printing mistake in one of the early descriptions"; most probable origin is Italian *casino* "a pleasure-house"... "public gambling-house"; Italian *cassino* means "box-cart". [S30]
- Typical scoring: "3 points are awarded for having the most cards, 2 for the ten of diamonds or great casino, 1 for the two of spades or little casino, 1 for the majority of spades, and 1 for each ace. Thus there are 11 points ... to which 1 point is added for each sweep." [S30]
- Reputation: "very simple and quite suitable for children, so that they may improve their counting ability"... yet "one of the games preferred by great gamblers". [S30]
- Earliest German mention: a book printed 1795 in Vienna and Prague (*Gesetzbuch der modernen Spiele Casino, Whist...*). German claim of Florentine origin from a gaming-house where nobles gathered (Schreiber 1854); Pratesi searched Florence's Casino dei Nobili archives without success. [S30]
- "Calling and building are important additional features absent from other games of the family and from traditional English Casino." Calling defined: "one plays a card to the table, earmarking one or more combinations of cards having that value; an intervening player may capture all if he, too, has a card of that value." [S30]
- Variants named: Royal Casino ("appears practically everywhere as the standard game"), Spade Casino, **Draw Casino** ("after the first distribution of cards, the pack is set on the table and each player takes the uppermost card from it on his turn"), **Give-away Casino** ("the player gaining the fewest points wins"). [S30]
- London sources: Robert Long, *Short Rules for Playing the Game of Casino* (1792); *A Mock-Heroic Poem... Laws of the Game of Casino* (1792, 1793); *Rules for Cassino* (1795, "Duchess of Marlborough"). "a craze for the game occurred in London in the early years of the 1790s". Early rules had no sweep points: "The name itself of sweep was limited to the clearing of the table at the end of the game − bringing no points − by the player who had been the last to take cards." Hoyle included Casino from 1796 (absent 1790). Literary refs: Jane Austen (1800) "tables of Whist and Cassino". [S30]
- Captain Crawley (1876): "Of this game I, personally, know nothing". *Popular Recreator*: "Cassino is a very pleasant and agreeable game, especially for young people." [S30]
- America: calling/building earliest in print "tentatively... New York 1866" (Parlett: Dick & Fitzgerald 1868); the old game called "Set-Back or Rounce Cassino"; 1892 American Hoyle has "21-Point Casino" and suggests scoring sweeps as they occur; Foster 1897 names Royal and Spade Casino and lists "Cassino-America" as a national game. USPCC 1900: Royal Casino with 11/12-pip cards (J13 Q14 K15 A1/16). Ostrow 1945: "This game is a hardy perennial whose popularity never wanes"; Spade Royal; **Diamond Casino** (3 cards dealt; 1 each for cards, majority of diamonds, 7♦, sweeps; 2 for all four 7s or 6s or Aces). Morehead *Modern Hoyle* 1944: "In the average home Casino is known as a children's game, but among gamblers it is known as the finest two-handed game of skill". Scarne: "greatest in popularity prior to the advent of Gin Rummy". [S30]
- Austria/Hungary: dealing by three and capturing a table card "by two in their hands (this being a unique feature)". [S30]
