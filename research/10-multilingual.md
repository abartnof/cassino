# 10 — Cassino/Kasino outside English: multilingual, non-Wikipedia sources

Scope: rules as described locally, local terminology and table talk, cultural notes, scoring variants, history claims. Coverage: Finnish, Swedish, Norwegian (Bokmål and Nynorsk), Danish, Icelandic, German (plus Zwicker), Polish, Hungarian, Russian, Italian (Scopa/Scopone), Portuguese/Brazil (plus Escopa/Escova), Spanish/Latin America (Casino, Escoba, Cuarenta), French/Québec, Japanese, Greek (Xeri), Afrikaans. Wikipedia was used only to find leads and is **never** cited below.

Conventions: original-language quotes come first, then *my translation* in italics. "OCR" marks text taken from machine-read scans, where spelling errors in the source are kept. "[snippet]" marks text read only as a full-text-search excerpt, because the book itself is lending-restricted.

## 1. Headline findings

- **The Nordic countries share one Kasino.** Finland, Sweden, Norway, Denmark and Iceland all play a form in which face cards have numbers (J=11, Q=12, K=13). Three cards are worth more from the hand: ♦10 = 16, ♠2 = 15, A = 14. Clearing the table (a "sweep") earns a bonus point with its own local name: *mökki* (FI), *tabbe* (SE/NO), *svupper*/older *svip* (DK), *svippur* (IS). [S1][S24][S28][S32][S37][S35][S39][S40]
- **The Finnish form, as Finnish sources describe it:**
  - The game is played to **16**.
  - **10 points** are dealt each round: most cards 1, most spades 2, ♦10 2, ♠2 1, aces 1 each.
  - A *mökki* earns a point, but if your opponent already has one, your new *mökki* "knocks down" (*kaataa*) theirs instead of scoring.
  - No *mökki* scores during the last deal.
  - Tied "most cards" or "most spades" points carry over to the next deal (they stay "in the deck").
  - Variants: *laistokasino* (misère), *rakennuskasino* (with building, played to 21), *pakkakasino* (refill your hand from the deck after each play). [S1][S28]
- **Sweep-cancelling and how the sweep is marked are Nordic design patterns worth copying.**
  - Finns, Swedes (in some *byggkasino* play) and Danes all let sweeps cancel each other.
  - Sweeps are marked physically. A captured card is turned face up in the pile. In Sweden the card is slid under the pile at a right angle, and each later *tabbe* card is slid in slightly offset from the one before, which makes a visible tally. [S1][S25][S37][S24]
- **Things said aloud are well documented in Swedish:**
  - *"sistan"*, *"båten går"*, *"sista given"*: the dealer's warning that this is the last deal.
  - *"bygger till knekt"*: "building to jack".
  - *"två åttor"*: "two eights", announcing a multiple build.
  - *"åttan ligger"* / the joking *"trött"*: "the eight lies" / "tired", saying you are adding to a build rather than capturing.
  - *"storan privat"*: "big casino private", a build to 16 that only ♦10 can take.
  - *"tabbe på knekt"*: "sweep on a jack", announced in misère.
  - *"river till sju"*: "knocking down to seven", reducing a build.
  - Nicknames for the casino cards: *storan/lillan*, *storstina/lillstina*, *lillkajsa/storkajsa*. [S24][S25][S26][S17]
- **There is a textual history across languages.** A Hoyle-type English Casino text was translated into German (1797). The same rules then appear in Polish (Wrocław, 1821) and Danish (1829). All three keep the English loanwords **"Sweep"** and **"Lurch"**, and the German and Polish books even give a pronunciation guide ("Swiep", "Lordsch"/"Świp", "Lordź"). [S41][S46][S36]
- **The earliest Nordic dates found:**
  - Swedish *cassino* as a card game: **1817** (Düben). [S16]
  - Swedish sweep term *tablerace* ("table rase"): **1838**. [S19]
  - Danish *svippe* ("clear the table at kasino"): **1852**. [S35]
  - Icelandic *kasína*: reportedly in Jón Árnason's collection of **1887**. [S40]
  - Finnish *mökki* in a kasino context: no earlier attestation found than **1938–39** (pulp magazines). A 1915 Finnish translation of Jack London renders "sweeps" without *mökki*. [S6][S7][S4]
- **The brief's premise about Brazil is not supported.** In 1996, Brazil's *Super Interessante* introduced casino as "radicalmente diferente dos carteados mais populares no Brasil" (*"radically different from the most popular card games in Brazil"*). The fishing game Brazilians actually play is **Escopa/Escova** (sum to 15), which Italian immigrants brought. [S49] Brazilian dictionaries do define *cassino grande/pequeno*. [S50][S51]

## 2. Finland (Kasino)

### 2.1 Rules as Finnish sources state them
- **Classification and audience.** Korttipeliopas (Mikko Saari, 2010) files Kasino under fishing games (*Kalastuspelit*) and says: "Kasino on hyvin suosittu peli. Se sopii lasten kanssa pelattavaksi, mutta myös aikuisille" (*"Kasino is a very popular game. It suits playing with children, but also adults"*). It calls the misère form *laistokasino* especially popular and points readers to the "Italian relatives" *scopone* and *scopa*. [S1]
- **Deal.** "Jakakaa neljä korttia pelaajille ja neljä pöytään kuvapuoli ylöspäin, tavallisesti kaksi korttia kerrallaan" (*"deal four cards to each player and four face up to the table, usually two at a time"*). Each turn "on pakko pelata kädestään pöytään yksi kortti" (*"you must play one card from hand to the table"*). [S1]
- **Card values.** "Kuvat ovat sotilas 11, kuningatar 12 ja kuningas 13. Ässä on kädessä 14, mutta pöydässä 1. Patakakkonen eli pieni kasino on kädessä 15 ja pöydässä tavallinen kakkonen, ruutukymppi eli iso kasino on kädessä 16 ja pöydässä 10." (*"Face cards are jack [lit. 'soldier'] 11, queen 12 and king 13. An ace is 14 in the hand but 1 on the table. The two of spades, or 'little casino', is 15 in the hand and an ordinary two on the table; the ten of diamonds, or 'big casino', is 16 in the hand and 10 on the table."*) [S1]
- **Mökki (sweep).**
  - "Jos saa yhdellä kortilla tyhjennettyä koko pöydän korteista, tekee mökin. Sen merkiksi laitetaan pistepinoon yksi otetuista korteista kuvapuoli ylöspäin." (*"If you empty the whole table with one card, you make a mökki ['cottage']. To mark it, one of the captured cards is put face up in your point pile."*)
  - "Jos kaksinpelissä vastapuolella on mökkejä, mökkiä ei oteta itselle vaan sen sijaan kaadetaan yksi vastapuolen mökeistä. Viimeisten korttien aikana, kun pakka on tyhjä, mökkejä ei enää lasketa." (*"In two-player play, if the opponent has mökkis, you don't take one yourself but instead knock down one of theirs. During the last cards, when the deck is empty, mökkis no longer count."*) [S1]
- **Scoring:** most cards 1; most spades (*patavoitto*) 2; each ace 1; ♦10 2; ♠2 1; each mökki 1.
  - The game goes to 16, and a player "voi kesken jaon lopettaa pelin, jos 16 pistettä tulee täyteen" (*"may stop the game mid-deal once 16 points are reached"*).
  - Tie-break order: "ensin lasketaan pistekorteista saadut pisteet, sitten kortit ja sitten padat" (*"first the points from point-cards, then cards, then spades"*).
  - Carry-over: "Jos korttivoitto tai patavoitto menee tasan, pisteet siirtyvät seuraavaan jakoon" (*"if the cards-win or spades-win is tied, the points move to the next deal"*). [S1]
- **21-point variant.** "Kaksinpelissä voi pelata myös niin, että jokaisesta yli seitsemän kerätystä padasta saa yhden lisäpisteen. Silloin hyvä erän pituus on 21 pistettä." (*"In two-player play you can also score one extra point for every spade beyond seven; then a good game length is 21."*) [S1]
- **Finnish face-card identity.** "Suomalaisessa kasinossa kuvakorteilla on pistearvot, muualla usein ei" (*"In Finnish kasino the face cards have numeric values; elsewhere often not"*). So in Finland a jack (11) can take a 4+7. [S1]
- **Rakennuskasino (building).** You announce aloud: "rakennan ässälle" (*"I'm building for the ace"*), meaning ace = 14.
  - "Jos pelaaja tekee kaksi rakennusta samalle numerolle, syntyy kaksoisrakennus… Kaksoisrakennusta ei voi enää korottaa." (*"Two builds to the same number make a double build, which can no longer be raised."*)
  - Scoring changes: "Rakennuskasinossa ei yleensä saa pisteitä mökeistä. Korttivoitosta saa 3 pistettä ja patavoitosta vain 1 pisteen. Yleensä pelataan 21 pisteeseen." (*"Usually no points for mökkis; cards-win 3, spades-win only 1; usually to 21."*)
  - On merging same-rank builds, players must "sanottava selvästi rakentaako kolmoselle vai kuutoselle" (*"say clearly whether they are building to three or to six"*). [S1]
- **Laistokasino (misère).** "Pelissä on luonnollisesti ottopakko… Vähiten pisteitä kerännyt voittaa." (*"Naturally there is a compulsion to capture… The one with the fewest points wins."*) [S1]
- **House rules from the site's comment thread (2014–2026):**
  - "Pimeä kasino": each player flips the top card of their hand instead of choosing one, "onnenpeli, johon perheen pienimmätkin voivat osallistua" (*"a game of luck in which even the smallest children can take part"*) (commenter Olli, 2014).
  - "Suppelan säännöt": "Mökkejä ei tuu enää jakoon, jos on menty jo 10 pisteeseen tai yli" (*"no more mökkis in a deal once someone has reached 10+"*) (2015).
  - Only the first mökki in a given hand counts, which "laskee onnen merkitystä" (*"reduces the role of luck"*) (Lauri, 2023).
  - The author agrees that the spade two counts toward spades (2024). [S1]
- **Educational value.** One commenter recommends Kasino for teaching children "numeroita ja yhteenlaskua, vatsinkin päässälaskua" (*"numbers and addition, especially mental arithmetic"*) (Tomi Blomberg, 2016). [S1]
- **Online quality warning.** The author dismisses Finnish online-casino affiliate pages that publish Kasino rules: they are "nettikasinomarkkinointisivu… On hyvin mahdollista, että säännöt on tehty generatiivisella tekoälyllä" (*"online-casino marketing sites… quite possibly generated with AI"*) (2025). This matches my own experience: most Finnish search hits are affiliate pages, and I did not use them. [S1]

### 2.2 Historical and literary attestations (Finnish National Library, digi.kansalliskirjasto.fi)
- **1915, Jack London in Finnish.** Työmies serialised a Finnish translation of London's "A Goboto Night" (*A Son of the Sun*). [S4][S5]
  - Deacon calls casino "lasten leikkiä" (*"child's play"*); Grief replies "Se on miesten peliä, kasino" (*"It's a man's game, casino"*). London's English has "a child's game" and "a bully game". [S4][S5]
  - "Luetaanko tasaparit?" (*"Do we count 'even pairs'?"*) translates London's "Do you count sweeps?", and "Noloa peliä se" (*"An embarrassing game, that"*) translates "That's a sissy game". [S4][S5]
  - "Kortit ensin ja padat ulos, tietysti, sitten pikku ja iso kasino ja ässät bridgen arwojärjestyksessä" (*"cards first and spades out, of course, then little and big casino and the aces in bridge order"*). London has "big and little". This is a count-out priority order. [S4][S5]
  - The translator glossed "pikku kasino (patakymppi)" (*"little casino (ten of spades)"*), which is wrong; the original means the two of spades. The same text glosses "iso kasino (ruutukymppi)" correctly. [S4]
  - **Inference, not a source claim:** in 1915 the established Finnish sweep term was apparently unknown to this translator.
- **1926, a Finnish rules book.** R. L. Sunderland, *Kaksitoista parasta korttipeliä*, "suomeksi sovittanut Väinö Meitti" (*"adapted into Finnish by Väinö Meitti"*), Karisto 1926. Its contents list includes "Kasino". [S14]
- **1930, "kahmaisu".** A translated crime story: "pelasimme casinoa iltamme ratoksi. Minulla oli kädessäni ruutukymppi, ja pöydässä oli seitsikko, kolmonen, kolme ässää, kakkonen ja viitonen. Olin juuri tekemäisilläni kahmaisun." (*"We played casino to pass the evening. I had the ten of diamonds in hand, and on the table were a seven, a three, three aces, a two and a five. I was just about to make a grab."*) *Kahmaisu* (grab or scoop) is an informal word for a big capture. [S8]
- **1931, Midsummer Eve gambling.** "Erääseen asuntoon Ätsärintie 3:ssa oli juhannusaattona kokoontunut suurehko joukko miehiä pelaamaan kasinoa. Peli oli uhkapeliä, ja panokset melko korkeita." (*"A fairly large group of men had gathered in a flat at Ätsärintie 3 on Midsummer Eve to play kasino. The game was gambling and the stakes fairly high."*) A cheating accusation ended in a shooting. [S9]
- **1932, a peaceful Sunday family game** (a Finnish emigrant writing from French Canada): "On paljon turvallisempaa suostua sunnuntai-illoin ukon ehdotukseen: »Eiköhän pelata casinoa?» Kun sitten istumme öljylampun loisteessa suuren pyöreän pöydän ympärillä, vallitsee rauhan henki talossa" (*"It is much safer to agree on Sunday evenings to the old man's suggestion: 'Shall we play casino?' When we sit in the oil-lamp's glow around the big round table, a spirit of peace reigns in the house."*) [S10]
- **1938, casino among gambling games.** A police-union magazine warns that athletes on competition trips "pelataan melkeinpä aina uhkapelejä, casinoa, »venttiä», pokeria" (*"almost always play gambling games: casino, 'ventti' [twenty-one], poker"*). [S11]
- **1938, earliest *mökki* attestation found.** A story in the "Taiga kertoo" series, titled "Iso casino", contrasts American casino with the local game:
  - "kun pelataan vanhaa, rehellistä casinoa — amerikkalaisittain, ilman mitään mökkejä, sieppauksia ja muuta maallista turhuutta, 11 pistettä erässä ja peli kahdestakymmenestäyhdestä poikki" (*"when you play old, honest casino — the American way, without any mökkis, snatches or other worldly vanity, 11 points a deal and game over at 21"*).
  - It also records building calls aloud: "»Kaksi kymppiä», hän sanoi" (*"'Two tens,' he said"*), then "»Kolme kymppiä… ja pikku casino joukossa.»" (*"'Three tens… and little casino among them'"*). The opponent takes the pile "isolla casinolla, jota muissa peleissä kutsutaan ruutukympiksi… »siitä tuli kolme poikaa ja hyvä alku patavoittoon«" (*"with big casino, which in other games is called the ten of diamonds… 'that made three boys [points] and a good start on spades'"*).
  - Final line: "tämä oli minulle iso casino" (*"this was a big casino for me"*), used as an idiom for a windfall. [S6]
- **1939, mökki again.** "He pelasivat casinoa. Hopkins pyörähdytti vielä mökinkin" (*"They played casino. Hopkins even whipped up a mökki too"*). [S7]
- **1923, summer kasino on the veranda.** A Swedish-language letter in Helsinki's Jewish youth paper *Hatchijo* describes the custom that "alla, fattiga och rika, resa ut till landet… har man dock alltid varit på humör att spela 'kasino' endera i verandan eller ute i det gröna" (*"everyone, poor and rich, goes out to the countryside… one has always been in the mood to play 'kasino', either on the veranda or out in the green"*). This is the earliest direct evidence I found of the Finnish summer-country (*mökki*-season) setting. [S12]
- **1924, Swedish-language table talk.** From a Finland-Swedish student magazine: "Isak och Calle spela kasino och använda termer, som låta underbara i oinvigdas öron. 'Nu har jag stora kasino', 'jo, och jag har lilla' hörs det allt med ens" (*"Isak and Calle play kasino and use terms that sound wonderful to the uninitiated. 'Now I have big casino', 'yes, and I have little' is heard all of a sudden."*) [S13]

### 2.3 Card-table sayings (Finnish folk tradition)
- These come from the Keuruu local archive, "Tauno Kuoppalan keräämiä sanontoja, joita on käytetty korttipelejä pelattaessa" (*"sayings collected by Tauno Kuoppala, used while playing card games"*). They are generic, not kasino-specific, and are said while slapping a card of a given suit on the table: [S2]
  - Spade: "Pata putos, muttei särkynyt." *"The pot fell but didn't break."* This is a pun: *pata* means both "spade" and "cooking pot".
  - Spade: "Patukat, sano Pinnin Jaska." *"Spades, said Pinni's Jaska."*
  - Diamond: "Ruudut suuret, sano lasimestari." *"Big panes, said the glazier."* Another pun: *ruutu* means "diamond" and "window-pane".
  - Club: "Ristin tie on kaitaa." *"The way of the cross is narrow."* Pun: *risti* means "club" and "cross".
  - Heart: "Herttasta oli Hanna-vainaan elämä." *"The late Hanna's life was all heart."*
  - **Game-design note:** these are ready-made, culturally authentic flavour lines that can be triggered by suit.

### 2.4 Popularity context and regional relatives
- **Popularity.** In 2024, 78.1% of Finns played card games at least sometimes (72.8% in 2022), and 33.4% played actively (at least monthly). The survey does not break out individual games. [S3]
- **Piitti.** *Pohjoiset korttipelit / Northern card games* (Ryytty & Säynäjäkangas, 2024; an updated bilingual edition of a 2002 book) covers eight northern Finnish games, including **piitti**. A regional newspaper review and the library catalogue confirm this; I did not get access to the game rules. [S15]

## 3. Sweden (Kasino, Byggkasino, Krypkasino, Mulle)

### 3.1 Lexicography and early attestations
- **SAOB, *kasino* sense II.1:**
  - "benämning på ett slags enkelt kortspel ('sällskapsspel')" (*"name of a kind of simple card game ('parlour game')"*).
  - First citation: Düben, *Talisman* 4:27 (**1817**).
  - "Cassino spelas med en hel kortlek, vanligen af fyra men stundom äfven af två eller tre personer. Hagdahl Fråga 28 (1883)." (*"Cassino is played with a whole deck, usually by four but sometimes also by two or three."*)
  - Under the compound: "Cassinospelet är af italienskt ursprung. Lindskog Spelb. 204 (1847)" (*"The cassino game is of Italian origin."*) This is an 1847 claim; see §16.
  - Spellings: "cassino 1817–1883". [S16]
- **SAOB sense II.2, the casino cards:**
  - "Två .. kort, som caracterisera Spelet, och hvilka bära namnet Cassino, nemligen Spader Tvåan och Ruter Tian. Düben Talism. 4:28 (1817)." (*"Two cards that characterise the game and bear the name Cassino, namely the two of spades and the ten of diamonds."*)
  - "Stora cassino (rutertia) räknas för 2 points… Lilla cassino, spader tvåa… räknas för 1 point. Hagdahl Fråga 28 (1883)." (*"Big cassino (ten of diamonds) counts 2 points… little cassino, two of spades… counts 1 point."*)
  - Colloquial forms: "Anm. Ifrågavarande kort kallas vard. äv. lillkasina resp. storkasina." (*"These cards are colloquially also called lillkasina and storkasina."*) [S16]
- **SAOB nicknames under *kajsa* and *lill-*:**
  - *kajsa* sense 5 is "en ombildning av KASINO med formell anslutning till namnet KAJSA" (*"a reshaping of KASINO after the woman's name Kajsa"*), found in *lill-kajsa* and *stor-kajsa*.
  - *lill-stina*, a further reshaping of *-kasina*, means "lilla kasino" (Östergren, 1931). [S17]
  - **Game-design note:** these are affectionate personifications, like Hungarian or English "Big/Little Casino". They suggest card "characters" in a game.
- **SAOB *tabbe* sbst. 2:** "[efter TABELRAS l. TABLE RASE] kortsp. i kortspelet kasino: förhållandet att ta hem alla kort på bordet. Werner o. Sandgren Kortox. 53 (1949)" (*"in the card game kasino: the act of taking home all the cards on the table"*). The other *tabbe*, sbst. 1, ordinarily means "blunder", so the sweep word is a homonym with a different etymology. [S18]
- **SAOB *tabelras*:**
  - "Den som gör tablerace får .. 1 point (i kasino). HbiblSällsk. 1:137 (**1838**)" (*"Whoever makes 'tablerace' gets 1 point (in kasino)."*)
  - Recorded forms: *tablerace* 1838, *tabelras* 1842, *table rase* 1845–1986, *tabberas* 1966.
  - Astrid Lindgren's Emil (1966) has the figurative use "Nu har vi tagit tabberas på allting" (*"Now we've made a clean sweep of everything"*). [S19]
- **Nordisk familjebok:**
  - 1st ed., vol. 3: "Casino (Kasino)… ett slags kortspel" (*"a kind of card game"*). [S20]
  - Uggleupplagan, vol. 13: "ett slags enkelt kortspel, som mest tjänar till nöje för barn" (*"a kind of simple card game, which mostly serves to amuse children"*). [S21]
  - 3rd ed., vol. 11: "ett slags enkelt kortspel". [S22]

### 3.2 Modern Swedish rules: Lyckans Talisman (2023–2026)
This is a well-sourced, ten-plus-book synthesis named after the old Swedish game book *Lyckans Talisman*. [S23][S24]

- **Hand-value choice (a Swedish-specific rule).**
  - "ruter tio som kallas för *storan* (även *storstina* eller *stora kasino*), spader två som kallas för *lillan* (*lillstina* eller *lilla kasino*)" (*"the ten of diamonds, called 'the big one' (also 'big Stina' or 'big casino'); the two of spades, called 'the little one'"*).
  - "Spelaren som står på tur får välja om storan skall räknas som sexton ögon eller de vanliga tio ögonen" (*"the player to move may choose whether the big one counts 16 or the usual 10"*). Each card has only one value at a time.
  - Variants: fixed values (16 in hand, 10 on table), or ♦10 and ♠2 never upgraded at all. [S24]
- **Sweep.** "Om en spelare tar in alla kort på bordet vid ett tillfälle, säger man att hen har gjort en *tabbe* eller *tabberas* (ytterst av latinska 'tabula rasa', rent bord)."
  - Marking: "spelaren skjuter in ett av de tagna korten med bildsidan upp under sin fångsthög, vinkelrätt mot högen. För varje ny tabbe skjuts ytterligare ett kort in under föregående tabbekort, något förskjutet mot detta." (*"the player slides one captured card face-up under the capture pile, perpendicular to it; for each new tabbe another card is slid in under the previous one, slightly offset."*) [S24]
  - **Game-design note:** this is a physical tally UI.
- **Calling the last deal.** "Innan givaren delar ut korten till den sista omgången, skall hen ange detta genom att säga 'sistan', 'båten går' eller 'sista given'." (*"Before dealing the last round the dealer must announce it by saying 'the last one', 'the boat's leaving' or 'last deal'."*) The optional point for the last capture is "*sistan* (även kallad *båten*)". [S24]
- **Scoring.** Most cards 1, most spades 2, *storan* 2, *lillan* 1, each ace 1, each *tabbe* 1. Ties score nothing. "Det är vanligt att spela ett parti som går först till 16 poäng" (*"usually first to 16"*). The spade count breaks ties. [S24]
- **Misère (*kasino misär*, *omvänd/avig kasino*).**
  - The *tvångstabbe* (forced sweep) rule carries a 5-point penalty if broken.
  - "En bra regel är att en spelare som spelar ett kort som möjliggör tabbe skall annonsera detta… bör säga 'tabbe på knekt'" (*"a good rule is to announce a card that enables a sweep, e.g. say 'sweep on a jack'"*).
  - Players with 21 or more are knocked out. [S24]
- **Dragkasino.** Draw from the stock so you always hold 4 cards. This is the same as Finnish *pakkakasino*. [S24][S1]
- **Byggkasino (building), with calls:** [S25]
  - "bygger till elva" / "bygger till knekt" (*"building to eleven / to jack"*).
  - For a composite build: "två åttor" (*"two eights"*).
  - Adding a same-rank card: "brukar spelaren förtydliga… genom att annonsera bygget med 'ligger' eller kortets valör och 'ligger'" (*"the player clarifies by announcing 'lies' or e.g. 'the eight lies'"*).
  - "Vissa spelare understryker att bygget bara kan tas in av detta enda kort genom att annonsera det med 'storan privat'" (*"some stress that a build to 16 can only be taken by that one card by announcing 'big one private'"*).
  - "tre tior" (*"three tens"*); "knekten ligger" (*"the jack lies"*).
  - Scoring: most cards **3**, spades **1**, game to **21**. A sweep-cancelling variant exists: "skall i första hand motparten annullera en av sina tabbar" (*"the opponent first cancels one of their own sweeps"*). [S25]
- **Origin note.** "Under andra halvan av 1800-talet introducerades möjligheten att bygga i kasino… i Sverige blev kasino med bygge ett eget spel som går under namnet byggkasino" (*"building was introduced in the second half of the 1800s… in Sweden casino with building became a separate game called byggkasino"*). [S23]
- **Mulle** (two decks, "twin" cards): [S26][S27]
  - Calls: "ligger", "åttan ligger", or "Ofta används också det lite skämtsamma uttrycket 'trött'" (*"often the slightly jokey 'tired' is used"*); "river till sju" (*"tearing down to seven"*, i.e. reducing a simple build); the last-deal call is "båten".
  - Bundling loose cards into a build is called *paketera* ("to package").
  - "Det är en särskild nesa att förlora med mer än 100 poängs marginal… Förloraren hamnar då i 'hundraklubben'. Denna förlust kallas ibland för 'senap'. Vinst med mer än 200 poängs marginal kallas… för 'ketchup' – då kommer allt på en gång." (*"It's a special disgrace to lose by more than 100: the loser joins 'the hundred club'; such a loss is sometimes called 'mustard'. A win by more than 200 is called 'ketchup' — then it all comes at once."*) [S26]
  - History: [S27]
    - Krypkasino is "omnämns första gången i en artikel i Dagens Nyheter 1932", with first rules in *Kortoxen* 1949. A sweep made with the twin card is a *dubbeltabbe*.
    - "mulle" as a casino game is first secure in Aftonbladet 1979.
    - Glimne's informant from Kumla prison: "Vet man inte vad mulle är efter en vistelse på t.ex. Kumla – har man inte 'suttit inne'!" (*"If you don't know what mulle is after a stay at Kumla, you haven't 'done time'!"*)
- **"Fishing" vocabulary (pagat, Swedish page by Martin Sörensson).** [S28]
  - "På svenska säger man att korten på bordet utgör *havet* och att spelarna *fiskar* upp kort därifrån" (*"In Swedish one says the table cards are 'the sea' and players 'fish' cards from it"*).
  - The dealer says "'sistan' eller 'båt'".
  - Discarding is *saka*.
  - Variant: "Kasino med överspader": one point per spade beyond six, usually played to 21. [S28]

## 4. Norway (Kasino; Nynorsk *storekasino / veslekasino*)

- **Current rules (norske-kortspill.no, 2026):** [S32]
  - Card names: "♦️10, storekasino… ♠️2, lillekasino".
  - Hand/table values: ♦10 16/10, ♠2 15/2, A 14/1, K/Q/J 13/12/11 both ways.
  - The dealer must say *"sisten"* at the last deal.
  - Building announcement: "Legger du en 5-er oppå en 4-er, sier du 'bygger 9'" (*"if you put a 5 on a 4, you say 'building 9'"*).
  - *Dobling* means putting a same-value card on a table card.
  - Sweep: *tabbe*, marked by turning a captured card face-up.
  - Scoring: ♦10 2, ♠2 1, each ace 1, most spades 1, most cards 1, *sisten* 1, each *tabbe* 1. First to 16; spades break ties.
  - Strategy tip: "Ligger det kort på bordet som til sammen blir 14, 15 eller 16, kan motstanderen hente hele haugen med et ess, en ♠️2 eller en ♦️10" (*"If table cards sum to 14, 15 or 16, the opponent can take the whole heap with an ace, ♠2 or ♦10"*).
  - History claim: "Spillet ble første gang beskrevet i England i 1792… Det sies ofte at det kommer fra Italia, men det er ikke funnet spor etter det der" (*"first described in England in 1792… often said to come from Italy, but no trace has been found there"*).
  - Equipment: "Papir og blyant til poengene" (*"paper and pencil for points"*).
  - Listed under "Påskespill" (*Easter games*). [S32]
- **The hytte (cabin) and a child scorekeeper (Nationen, 12 March 1985).** In a snowbound mountain cabin: "Og så spiller vi om storekasino og lillekasino og fire ess og flest kort og spar. Helst skal det være noen «tabber» og guttungen fører regnskapet og teller sammen og leser opp stillingen. Det kan gå timer med slik underholdning." (*"And then we play for big casino and little casino and four aces and most cards and spades. Preferably there should be some 'tabber', and the little boy keeps the accounts, adds up and reads out the score. Hours can pass with such entertainment."*) [S29]
  - **Game-design note:** a social role (child as scorekeeper who reads the score aloud) and the cabin setting, parallel to the Finnish *mökki* setting.
- **Nynorsk translation of the same Jack London story (1929).** "Eg gaar ut paa veslekasino og dei fire essa. Med storekasino og «spar» kjem De berre til tjuge" (OCR: "veslskasino"; *"I go out on little casino and the four aces. With big casino and 'spades' you only reach twenty"*), "siste stikk", "flest kort". [S30]
- **General-knowledge status.** A 2008 Norwegian quiz: "Hvilket kort kalles «storekasino» i spillet kasino?" (*"Which card is called 'big casino' in the game kasino?"*). [S31]

## 5. Denmark (Kasino; *svupper*, *sidsten*, spar 5)

- **DDO (Den Danske Ordbog) sense 2:** "kortspil for to-fire deltagere hvor hver deltager skal forsøge at parre eller kombinere kortene på hånden med de kort der ligger åbne på bordet" (*"card game for two to four players in which each tries to pair or combine hand cards with the open table cards"*). Grammar: "uden pluralis". [S33]
- **ODS (Ordbog over det danske Sprog), vol. 10:** "2) … dels: navn paa et kortspil; dels i forb. lille og store kasino, navn paa spar to og ruder ti i dette spil" (*"name of a card game; also in 'little and big casino', names of the two of spades and ten of diamonds in this game"*). Cited sources: J. Baden's foreign-word dictionary, "GlSpil.24ff.", and Ellen Reumert (1919). [S34]
- **ODS, vol. 22 (historical sweep vocabulary):**
  - "Svip… i forb. *tage svip*, i spillet kasino: tage rent op af bordet, saa der ingen kort ligger fremme. GlSpil.26." (*"'take svip': in kasino, take everything off the table so that no cards lie exposed"*)
  - "svippe… tage rent op af bordet i spillet kasino. Holmgaard.Spillebog.(**1852**).38." [S35]
- **1829 Danish game book (*Nyeste dansk spillebog*), "XV. Cassinospillet":** [S36]
  - It follows the same Hoyle-derived text as the German 1797 book: "Kongerne, Damerne og Knegterne kaldes Hoffort (Court Cards)".
  - "Den, som tager det sidste Bordkort eller gjør Bordet ledig, tæller for hver gang dette skeer, uigienkaldelig en Point" (*"whoever takes the last table card or makes the table empty scores, each time, irrevocably one point"*).
  - "dette kalder man Svep eller Kjørud" (*"this is called Sweep or Kjørud"*); "Lurchen… eller dobbelt vundet Spil er Fem". [S36]
- **Modern Danish rules (ludo.dk; spilregler.dk):**
  - Values: "Ruder 10 ('Store kasino') kan bruges som værdi 10 eller 16. Spar 2 ('Lille kasino')… 2 eller 15", aces 1 or 14.
  - Uniquely: "**Spar 5 rydder bordet**" (*"the five of spades clears the table"*).
  - Sweep and last deal: "Hvis du tager et stik og bordet bliver helt tomt bagefter, har du lavet en 'svupper'… Markér en svupper ved at vende ét kort i din stikbunke"; "Kortgiveren markerer den sidste hånd ved at udmelde 'sidsten'".
  - Build calls: "bygger ni", and "en spiller kan lægge en 7 oven på en 7 og melde '**mere syv**'" (*"announce 'more seven'"*).
  - "De fleste spiller med at 'svuppere' udligner hinanden" (*"most play that sweeps cancel each other"*).
  - Scoring: spades 2, cards 1, ♦10 2, ♠2 1, aces, *sidsten* 1, each *svupper*. "Der spilles traditionelt… 21 point". [S37][S38]
- **Note on "svupper".** The everyday Danish word *svupper* means a sink plunger, per bilingual phrasebooks in the archive.org full-text search. This is a lexical aside, not verified in a fetched dictionary.

## 7. German (Casino 1797; Zwicker)

- **1797 translation of the English rules (*Das neue Königliche l'Hombre… Casino*), pp. 316–330:** [S41]
  - Name claim: "Die Benennung Cassino kömmt von a[inem Hause] in Florenz her worinn sich der Adel versammlet" (OCR damaged; *"The name Cassino comes from a [house] in Florence where the nobility assembles"*).
  - Values: face cards only pair. "Das große Cassino, welches die Zehen von Caro ist… zwey Points… Mit dem kleinen Cassino… die Zwei von Piques".
  - Sweep: "Derjenige, der die letzte Tischkarte nimmt, oder den Tisch räumt, zähle für jedesmal unwiderruflich ein Point" (*"whoever takes the last table card or clears the table scores one point irrevocably each time"*).
  - Last cards: "dieses nennet man den Sweep oder den Kehraus", with a footnote telling readers to pronounce it "wie Swiep" and *Lurch* "wie Lordsch".
  - Scoring: 11 points (cards 3, big casino 2, little 1, aces 4, spades 1). The loser's points are subtracted from the winner's ("die geringere Zahl wird von der größern abgezogen"). A "Lurch (Bredouille)" (double game) is five.
  - Scorekeeping: "Man marquirt auf folgende Art", followed by a printed pattern of counters.
  - Three-handed: to 15, with a priority order for counting out: "Erstlich das große Casino, 2tens das kleine Casino, 3tens die Mehrheit der Karten, 4tens die Mehrheit der Piques, 5tens das Pique-As, 6tens das Trefle-As, 7tens das Coeur-As, 8tens das Caro-As".
  - Strategy rule 9: "Wenn man in seiner Hand ein As und das kleine Casino hält… so thut man besser, man nimmt [spielt] das letztere" (*"if you hold an ace and little casino and must play one, play the latter"*), because the opponent gains only 1 point instead of possibly 2.
  - The same text appears in Martius, *Unterricht in der natürlichen Magie*, vol. 19 (1805), p. 259 [snippet]. [S42]
- **No modern dictionary sense.** DWDS gives *Kasino/Casino* only as clubhouse, officers' mess and gaming house; there is no card-game sense. [S43]
- **Zwicker/Zwickern** (Schleswig-Holstein relative): [S44]
  - "Dann legt er vier Karten offen in die Mitte. Diese Karten bezeichnet man als Bild" (*"the open cards are called 'the picture'"*).
  - Capturing is *stechen*. "Sticht er alle Karten, spricht man von einem Zwicker" (*"if he takes all the cards it's called a Zwicker"*).
  - Stacking is allowed up to 14. Scoring: most cards 1, each ace 2, ♦7 and ♠7 1 each, ♦10 10, each Zwicker 3. [S44]
  - Pagat's informant-based version, with jokers worth 15/20/25 and "Zwick" ~ English "tweak", is summarised in [S45].

## 11. Italy (Casino is not a card name; Scopa/Scopone)

- **Treccani *casino*:** "casa signorile di campagna"; "c. dei nobili, c. da gioco" (club, gaming house); no card-game sense. This supports the 1797 and 1821 claim that the name points to Italian aristocratic clubs, not to an Italian game. [S47][S41][S46]
- **Chitarrella's scopone rules (Neapolitan with an Italian version, tradition dated 1750; ed. Alfonso Grasso):** [S48]
  - Etymology: "la scopa prende il suo nome dal punto che si fa prendendo tutte le carte in tavola, come se la si scopasse" (*"scopa is named for the point made by taking all the table cards, as if sweeping the table"*).
  - Marking: "mette questa carta, voltata, sotto al mazzo… in modo che si veda. Chesto se chiamma: fare na scopa" (*"he puts the card face-up under his pile so it shows: this is called 'making a scopa'"*).
  - Key rule difference from Cassino: "se in tavola c'è l'uguale, non si può fare la somma delle altre" (*"if a matching card is on the table you may not take a sum instead"*).
  - Points of the deck: cards, coins (*denari*), "Settebello" / "sett'oro", primiera (7=21, 6=18, A=16…).
  - Table jargon:
    - *mulinello* ("windmill"): one team keeps capturing.
    - *fare il quarantotto* ("make the forty-eight"): deduce the remaining hands.
    - *giocare contro la quarta* ("play against the fourth"): risk a scopa when one card remains unseen.
    - *cappotto*: shutout.
    - *fare il giro*: rotating partners.
    - *cartaro/mazziere*: dealer.
  - Proverbial ending: "Chi non ha memoria… lasci lo Scopone e vada a giocare a noci" (*"whoever lacks memory should leave Scopone and go play with walnuts"*). [S48]

## 13. Spanish / Latin America

  - "Cada escoba, se marcará colocando una carta boca arriba en el momento de recoger la baza" (*"each sweep is marked by placing a card face up when gathering the trick"*).

**Patterns visible in the table:**
1. Only the Nordic family gives ♦10, ♠2 and aces higher capture values from the hand.
2. Nordic sweeps usually score 1 and may cancel each other.
3. The English-derived continental texts (German, Polish, Danish, Russian, Brazilian, Argentine) keep "pair-only" face cards and cards = 3 points.
4. The ♦10 survives as a point card even in unrelated games: Greek Xeri and German Zwicker.

## 18. Design takeaways for a video game (sourced)

- **Announcements as UI events:**
  - Last deal: "sistan / båten går" (SE), "sisten" (NO), "sidsten" (DK), "últimas cartas" (PT).
  - Build declarations: "rakennan ässälle" (FI), "bygger till knekt" / "två åttor" / "ligger" / "storan privat" (SE), "bygger 9" (NO), "bygger ni" / "mere syv" (DK), "Строю 7" (RU), "construindo seis" (PT).
  - Misère warning: "tabbe på knekt" (SE).
  - Sources: [S1][S24][S25][S32][S37][S57][S49]
- **Visible sweep tally:**
  - SE: offset cards slid under the pile. [S24]
  - FI/NO/DK: a card turned face up. [S1][S32][S37]
  - Iceland's "borðskeiningur" is scored separately "i Regnskabet". [S39]
- **Sweep cancellation**, an anti-snowball mechanic: FI *kaataa*; SE byggkasino variant; DK "udligner". [S1][S25][S37]
- **Score-reading role:** in the 1985 Norwegian cabin, the child "fører regnskapet… og leser opp stillingen" (*"keeps the accounts… and reads out the standings"*). [S29]
- **Score-chant feature:** Hungarian "Laptöbbség! pikktöbbség! nagy k., kis k., a négy ász". [S56]
- **Suit-slap flavour lines:** Finnish puns. [S2]
