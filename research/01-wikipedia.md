## 0. Headline findings (for the game team)

- **Nine editions describe nine different games.** Point values, the target score, whether building is allowed, whether court cards have numeric values, how sweeps work, and the deal size all differ by edition (see the comparison table in §3). Two families stand out:
  - **Anglo-American Cassino** (en, it, ja): court cards only pair, building is allowed, 11 points per deal (3 for cards, 1 for spades), game to 11 or 21.
  - **Nordic/German "Royal-style" Kasino** (sv, fi, nb, da, de): J/Q/K = 11/12/13. Aces, ♠2 and ♦10 have a higher value when played *from hand* (14/15/16). Most-spades is often worth 2 and most-cards 1. A sweep is a *tabbe* (sv/nb), *mökki* (fi) or *svupper* (da), and sweeps cancel each other out. Game to 16 (sv/fi) or 21 (da/de).
- **Hungarian Kaszinó is unusual.** Deals are 3 cards each. A player may combine *several hand cards* against several table cards, up to a maximum sum of 13. After a sweep (*tábla*) the opponent must trail and then *moves again*. The last round is played with open hands. The game ends when a player calls „Ausz!” or „Kint vagyok!” ("I'm out!"), and a capture can be challenged with „Fals!” [S20, S73].
- **Announcements are a core mechanic, not decoration.** The 1868 *American Hoyle* (verified on archive.org) requires builds to be announced in the **singular** ("Seven", "Nine", "Ten") and calls in the **plural** ("Fours", "Fives", "Treys"). It requires them "audibly and distinctly", or the play is void [S79]. Every edition has its own spoken formulae (see §5).
- **Origin claims have shifted over time, and the history shows how.** Wikipedia editions have variously claimed that the game came from Italy (en before 2019; nb still does), 17th-century France (de until 2022), 15th-century France as a gambling game (ja 2007–2013), and **China** (sv, today). The en and de articles now follow Pratesi (1995): the game is English and first recorded in 1792, and there is no evidence for an Italian origin [S1, S3, S16, S25, S29].
- **Scorekeeping aids appear throughout.** Sweeps are recorded by turning a captured card face up or crosswise (all editions). Foster's 1897 Hoyle scores the game with "counters, on a sheet of paper, or on a cribbage board", and Spade Cassino is pegged to 61 [S78]. Cuarenta (Ecuador) uses chips called *tantos* and *perros* [S44]. Swedish Wikibooks documents a computer Kasino with "Bygg"/"Bygg mera" buttons and a `*x*` marker for a multiple build [S36].
- **Popularity data.** Pageviews from Sep 2025 to Aug 2026: en 106/day, **nb 79/day**, **fi 66/day**, da 40/day, sv 34/day, hu 21/day, de 6/day, ja 3.5/day. Every Nordic edition peaks strongly in **July**, the summer-cottage season [S63].

## 2. Edition by edition

### 2.1 English — *Cassino (card game)* (rev 1375945654, 2026-09-21) [S1]

**Classification and summary**
- Infobox: Family "Matching"; type "Fishing"; players "2–4 (2 best)"; 52 cards; origin "England"; related "Skwitz • Tablanette • Zwickern"; playing time "10–15 min."; random chance "Medium"; skills "Tactics" [S1].
- "'''Cassino''', sometimes spelt '''Casino''', is an English card game for two to four players … It is played with slight variations in various parts of the world, sometimes also under the names of Wippen, Basra, Tablanette and Pasur." [S1]
- "Cassino is the only fishing game to have penetrated the English-speaking world. It is similar to the Italian game of Scopa and is often said, without substantiation, to be of Italian origin." Cited to Parlett (2008), p. 401 [S1]. *Parlett's text was not accessible; archive.org has the book only as a borrowable copy with no text.* Pagat makes the same point: "Casino is the only fishing game to have become popular in English speaking countries. Although it is traditionally supposed to have originated in Italy, there is no direct evidence of it having been played there" [S64].

**History claims, with verification status**
- "As 'Cassino', the game is first recorded in 1792 in England … where it appears to have become something of a fashionable craze". Cited to Long (1792) and Pratesi [S1]. Long's *Short Rules for Playing the Game of Cassino* (1792) is on archive.org as `bim_eighteenth-century_short-rules-for-playing-_long-robert_1792`. The OCR is badly garbled, but I could read fragments: "Never [lay down a Ten] while Great Cassino is in, nor a Deuce [while] Little Cassino is [out]"; "When obliged to lay down a Card, let it be a Court-Card, or [a] little one, but keep the Aces"; "The Ten of Diamonds, which is [called Great Cassino] … Points"; "The Majority of the Cards three Points"; "Partners like the Game of Whist" [S81].
- The quote "I do long for a game of Cassino" (Mrs. Scatter in Frederick Reynolds's *Cheap Living*, 1797) is **verified**. The archive.org scan reads: "Mrs. Scat. … I do long for a game of caſſino —that is, in the family way — juſt for a trifle; —I never loſe much, you know." It appears on pp. 58–59 of this printing; Wikipedia cites p. 39, presumably another edition [S76].
- "At that stage, the court cards had no numerical value and could only be paired, and there was no building; that did not appear in English rules until the second half of the century." Cited to "Trumps" 1868 [S1]. *Not verified against that source.*
- The counting cards in Long are "the Aces and two special cards known as the 'Great Cassino' (♦10) and 'Little Cassino' (♠2)" [S1].
- Austria-Hungary: "in 1795 in Vienna and Prague, rules were published that incorporated English terminology such as 'sweep' and 'lurch.'" By 1810 a new variant gave court cards 11/12/13, aces 1 or 14, Great Cassino 10 or 16, and Little Cassino 2 or 15 (von Abenstein 1810) [S1].
  - **Verified in the 1810 *Spielalmanach*** (BSB copy on archive.org, `10431527bsb`). The game is "unter vier Personen gespielt … wie beim Whist" [played by four persons … as at whist]. A Queen "welche 12 gilt" [which counts 12]; an Ace taken "nach dem Werthe von 14" [at the value of 14]; the great Casino "nach ihrem Casino-Werthe von 16" [at its Casino value of 16].
  - The terminology passage reads: "dieses nennt man den Sweep, oder den Kehraus" [this is called the Sweep, or the *Kehraus*, "sweep-out"]; "Der Lurch (Bredouille) wird das doppelt gewonnene Spiel genannt" [the Lurch (*Bredouille*) is the name for the doubly-won game].
  - It also gives a **pronunciation note** (OCR noisy): German readers who keep the English terms "müssen das Wort Sweep … lesen und aussprechen wie Schwiep; und das Wort Lurch, wie Lordsch" [must pronounce "Sweep" like *Schwiep* and "Lurch" like *Lordsch*].
  - It uses **"table nette (Raffel)"** for clearing the table: a player may prefer to leave two cards rather than one, "damit sein Nachfolger nicht etwa den Tisch räume, oder table nette (Raffel) mache" [so that his successor doesn't clear the table, or make *table nette (Raffel)*].
  - The 1810 text includes a **scorekeeping diagram** of circles for marking points ("Man markirt auf folgende Art:" [one marks in the following way:]); the OCR renders it as rows of "O"s. Clearing-the-table points "werden jedesmal schon während des Spieles markirt und nie gegenseitig abgezogen" [are marked during play and never deducted from each other] [S83].
- Lady Sarah Nicolas (1846): "the game of Cassino is thus played in some parts of Germany:- Great Cassino takes sixteen. Little Cassino – fifteen. Every Ace – fourteen. King – thirteen. Queen – twelve. Knave – eleven." **Verified** in *The Cairn* (1846), p. 128: "THE game of Cassino is thus played in some parts of Germany : — Great Cassino takes sixteen. Little Cassino— fifteen. Every Ace — fourteen. King — thirteen. Queen — twelve. Knave — eleven." [S77]
- American developments named in the article: building and calling; **Royal Cassino** (court values); **Spade Cassino**; **Diamond Cassino** (3-card deal). Royal Cassino is first mentioned in 1894 (a ship passenger), and its rules first appear in *Foster's Complete Hoyle* (1897). "Cassino was eventually eclipsed by Gin Rummy." [S1]
- "By the early 20th century, Cassino itself was obsolete everywhere, but two successors were emerging", namely Zwickern and Tablanette [S1].
- Myths of origin: "detailed research by Franco Pratesi has shown that there is no evidence of it ever being played in Italy and the earliest references to its Italian cousins, Scopa and Scopone, post-date those of Cassino." "Likewise an origin in gambling dens appears unlikely since a casino in the late 18th century was a summer house or country villa" (Thompson 2015, p. 43) [S1]. *Neither Pratesi (1995) nor Thompson was accessible; Google Books blocked automated access.*

**Rules as given in the article**
- English Cassino (after Long 1792): 2 players, 4 cards each and 4 to the table. Example: with table cards 6–9–3–9–J, "by playing a 9, a player may capture the 6 and 3 in combination as well as two 9s." Clearing the table ("later called a sweep") scores 1. Captured cards are kept face up. Leftover cards go to the last capturer, "unless the last card played is a court card in which case its holder claims any remaining cards" [S1].
- Scoring table: Most cards 3, Great Cassino 2, Little Cassino 1, Most spades 1, each Ace 1, each sweep 1. "Thus there are 11 points in the game plus the bonus points for sweeps … The lower score is deducted from the higher … Game is 11 points; if the loser has less than 6, he or she is lurched and loses double." [S1]
  - **Cross-check with Hoyle's Games Improved (1796):** "the Points consist of eleven, and the Lurch is six". It lists Great Cassino 2, Little Cassino 1, four Aces 4, Majority in Spades 1, and "The Sweep or last Trick". In 1796, then, "sweep" also named taking the last cards: "The Player who obtains the last Trick sweeps all the Cards on the Table" [S82].
- Three-handed: "the two lower scores are combined and deducted from the highest". Four-handed: Whist conventions, a rubber of three games [S1].
- American Cassino ("Trumps" 1867): 2, 3, 4 or 6 players. Features: capturing (pairing or combining), sweeping, **calling** ("call their rank e.g. 'Fives'"), **building** ("he places the 3 on the 2 and calls 'Five'"), multiple build, second build, build-and-call ("call their values e.g. 'Eights'"), trailing. "Each deal is a complete game." [S1]
  - **Verified in the American Hoyle (1868 printing, pp. 217–222), which is stronger than Wikipedia's summary:** "It will be observed that a player, in announcing the denomination of a build, always employs the singular number. Thus: 'Nine' or 'Ten' — not 'Nines' or 'Tens.' This is called building up." For calls: "In calling the denomination, the plural is always used. Thus: 'Fours,' not 'Four.' This is termed calling."
  - Law 12: "the player must declare the denomination of the proposed 'build' or 'call,' audibly and distinctly … failing to comply with this requirement, his opponent may separate the cards". The text adds "the mere act of playing a Five on a Deuce does not of itself constitute a 'build' … unless the player of the Five says … audibly and distinctly, 'Seven'".
  - The 1868 text **opens by asserting an Italian origin**: "Cassino is a card game of Italian origin, and is fast becoming a favorite in this country". Its laws "received the approval of the best players in this city".
  - It calls the European 11-point game "Set-back or Bounce Cassino", and the misdeal penalty is "forfeiture of the game" [S79].
- Variants in the article:
  - **Twenty-one Point Cassino**, first printed in Dick's 1880 *Modern Pocket Hoyle*: "Cassino is now very generally played for a fixed number of points (usually twenty-one)". Sweeps are scored as taken, and "A player who erroneously claims to have won loses the game" [S1]. *The 1868 Modern Pocket Hoyle on archive.org does not contain the 21-point rule, which is consistent with a later first appearance; I could not verify the 1880 text* [S80]. **Foster (1897) confirms the claiming rule:** "The moment he reaches 21 he should claim the game, and if his claim is correct he wins … If he is mistaken, and cannot show out, he loses the game" [S78].
  - **Royal Cassino** (Foster 1897): J=11, Q=12, K=13. Parlett adds the Ace as 1 or 14 [S1]. **Foster verified:** "The Jack is worth 11, the Queen 12, and the King 13; so that a 9 and 2 can be taken in with a Jack, or a 6 4 and 3 with a King." [S78]
  - **Spade Cassino**: "every spade scored a point except for the ♠J which scored 2 … gave 24 points per hand … Game is 61 … scored on a cribbage board" [S1]. **Foster verified:** "every spade counts one point … The spade Jack counts one in addition … 24 points are made in every hand, exclusive of sweeps … The game is scored on a cribbage board, every point being pegged immediately … Sixty-one points is game, once round the board and into the game hole." [S78] (The it edition and the gambiter mirror say "25", which disagrees with Foster's 24; see §2.6.)
  - **Diamond Cassino**: a "cross between Cassino and Scopa", 40 cards, 3 each, 4 to the table, game 11. Scoring: most cards 1, most diamonds 1, ♦7 1, "2 for all four 7s, 6s or As", 1 per sweep (Parlett 2008 p. 404) [S1].
  - **Draw Cassino**, first called Royal Draw Cassino (1911), draws a replacement after each play; two players [S1].
  - Related games in the article: Callabra ("fast and simple forerunner of Cassino"; 3 each and 5 to the table; J/Q/K 11/12/13); Tablanette ("said to be of Russian provenance", 6 cards, a jack clears the table); Diloti (Greek: most cards 4, ♦10 2, ♠2 1, each ace 1, "Each sweep (Xeri): 10 (!) extra points") [S1].

### 2.2 English Talk page — disputes and lore [S2]
- **The picture dispute (2009):** "It is obvious that the game being played in the picture is not Cassino. There are 12 cards on the table … it very, very rarely gets above eight or so … in Cassino, placing a card partially covering another marks it as a 'build'" (IP 91.105.26.197, 2009-05-07). The picture was removed. *This is a practical heuristic for UI design: table size in real play rarely exceeds about 8 cards.*
- **Classification (2010):** "Cassino is not a comparing type card game. It is a fishing game! I have corrected this several times … Stop changing this!" (Suttkus).
- **Origin of the name (2012/2022):** "I once read in a book that is a mis-spelling of 'Casino' and the mis-spelling stuck" (ACEOREVIVED). Bermicourt replied in 2022: "possibly named after the generic Italian word casino which, in those days, meant a summer house or country villa. It's the sort of game that might be played at such a place … Both spellings were used early on, but 'Casino' was first." (This conflicts with the current article, which says "Cassino" is used in the earliest rules of 1792; see §6.)
- **Diamond Cassino:** "I believe that it is an Italo-American version of Scopa" (ACEOREVIVED, 2012).
- **South African Cassino (2020, Sanelerhoo):** "not played with the picture cards … it is common played in Prisons and is liked by gangsters and tarven or pub goers … Players are dealt Ten cards each … Cards are not dealt to the table centre face up". Scoring "each ace … 1 point, Two of Spades … 1 point, Ten of diamonds … 2 points, 6 card with Spades (+5♠️) recieve 3 points and player(parners) with more cards receive 1 point", game 11. Reply: "unless it is cited we can't include it. Try contacting pagat.com" (Bermicourt, 2023).
- **Regional scoring (2026, Keith Ammann):** "3 points for Great Cassino (my father called it 'Big Cassino'), 2 points for Little Cassino, 1 point per ace, 1 point each for most cards and most spades, and no bonus for sweeps … Pagat … describes 3 for Big/2 for Little/1 for cards as 'a version played in prisons in Connecticut' (LOL) … he did spend the latter part of his youth in the Hudson Valley". **Checked against the current pagat page:** it now reads "I have two reports of a version played by some in Connecticut and New York state in which 3 points are scored for the 10 of diamonds, 2 points for the 2 of spades and just 1 point for taking most cards". The prison wording is no longer there [S64].
- **2015 IP:** "sweeps are only scored in three and four person games, and play is typically to 25, but occasionally 50 or 100" (unsourced) [S2].

### 2.3 English page history: removed content and origin edit-warring [S3–S10]
This material was once on Wikipedia and is now gone, mostly because it lacked sources. **It is folk testimony, not verified fact**, but it is a rich source of terminology.
- **First version (2004)** [S4]: builds were announced "by saying 'building tens', 'building sixes', or whatever the sum is … one player places a deuce onto a three … and says 'building fives' … The next player may place another deuce on the pile and say 'building sevens'." Leftover cards at the end: "These cards ('sweeps') are taken by the player who took the last trick."
- **Alternate card names** (ScreaminEagle, 2006-12-13): "10 of diamonds ('big cassino' or 'big ten')"; "2 of spades: ('little cassino,' 'little deuce' or 'spy two')" [S10].
- **Pre-2023 text** (rev 1154273302, parked and then removed by Bermicourt on 2023-05-11) [S5]:
  - The dealer announces **"cards"** when dealing the last cards.
  - "♦10 ('big cassino', 'big ten', or 'good ten'): 2"; "♠2 ('little cassino', 'little deuce', 'good two', or 'spy two'): 1".
  - "building nine", "building sevens"; "multiple building," "natural building", or "double building".
  - Face cards pair one at a time, which "removes the possibility of a so-called 'orphan' face card remaining and preventing further sweeps."
  - "It is common for the dealer, if dealt a face-card in the final hand, to hold this until the final play".
  - **All 11 points:** "some rules state that this player will be awarded an extra point for 12 total points … Other rules state that this is a 'skunk' if it occurs in the first round".
  - **Sudden-death scoring:** sweeps scored as they occur end the round if the sweeper reaches 20+. "In a variation devised in Michigan … a player who defaults on the duty to capture after building gives up 2 points … In a series of 'rounds to 5,' any three instant scores … against the same player ends the round."
  - **Initial Pairs:** pairs dealt to the table go automatically to the first player.
  - **Five players:** "removing the deuces of hearts, diamonds and clubs".
  - **Strategy text:** "Building exists as a means of protecting cards … The value of building decreases significantly as the number of players in the game increases. In a two-player game, one requires only one adversary to be bereft of the necessary cards; in a four-player game, one requires three". Three "Variants" govern what a player with a build on the table may do; "While Hoyle recommends variant 1, all variants are very common in different regions."
- **Portuguese Cassino** (added 2018, removed 2023): game to 31; "10 of diamonds ('menina')" (an earlier revision spelled it "manina"); sweep = *clareza*; a 26–26 tie is decided by spades. Bermicourt replaced "clearance" with "sweep": "let's not add an unofficial term" [S5, S7, S10].
- **California Cassino** (merged 2009, removed 2022 as "No reliable source found"): uses the verbs "Laying/Pairing/Adding/Building"; no sweep points; "each player must deal twice in a complete match" [S6].
- **Pluck Cassino** (2018): "Each player has one opportunity each time cards are dealt to 'pluck' a card from the table … Point cards cannot be plucked" [S7].
- **Florida Cassino** (Krusty24, 2008; reverted as "made up, zero google hits"): subtractive building, "Mega Sweeps" (playing several hand cards to sweep, then sitting out turns), and reversed Big/Little values [S9].
- **Trailing Royals Cassino** (2008) [S10]. **"5s sweep rule"** (2007): "trailing the five of spades sweeps the table, giving one point". This matches the Danish rule (§2.4) [S10].
- **South Africa American cassino** (Sanelerhoo, Feb 2024; removed June 2024): "It is Familiar with ex-con cause it is a popular game in prison alongside Murabaraba, Dices and Ludo … Some People play it in Train commuter … Most played in Tavern and shibins". 40 cards, 10 each. Defines a "FULL" build. Scoring: most cards 3, ♦10 2, ♠2 2, most spades 1, each ace 1 [S8].
- **Origin edit-war timeline:**
  - 2006-07-06, Suttkus: "It is similar to and probably descended from the Italian game Scopa" (and removed the Italian category).
  - 2007-06-21, Aldaron removed "'Casino' is the official name … 'Cassino' is a common mis-spelling. Source: Official Hoyle Rulebook" with the summary "Hoyle is not a modern authority on the history of games; see Parlett".
  - 2019-11-24, Bermicourt: "Since there is no evidence of an Italian link it is misleading to have an image with Italian-suited cards".
  - 2020-11-06: "no evidence of Italian provenance".

### 2.4 Danish — *Kasino (kortspil)* (rev 12158922) [S11–S13]
- Tagged "ingen kilder" (no sources, January 2021). The image shows "Kortgivning til Kasino for to spillere" [dealing for two players] [S11].
- **Deal:** 4 each and 4 face up, "Traditionelt uddeles to kort af gangen" [traditionally dealt two at a time] [S11].
- **Actions:** *Udsmidning* (trailing), *Matchning*, *Kombinere*, *Bygning*. Taking is optional: "Man SKAL ikke tage stik, hvis man kan" [you are NOT obliged to take a trick if you can] [S11].
- **Sweep = "svupper":** "Hvis en spiller rydder bordet … kaldes det en 'svupper', og dette giver 1 point … Pointet markeres ved, at spilleren vender det øverste kort i sin stik bunke." [If a player clears the table it is called a 'svupper' and gives 1 point; it is marked by turning the top card of one's trick pile.] "De fleste spiller med at bordrydninger (svuppere) udligner hinanden." [Most play that sweeps cancel each other.] [S11]
- **Spoken calls:**
  - The dealer announces the last round: "Den sidste runde markeres af kortgiveren ved at udmelde 'sidsten'" [the dealer marks the last round by announcing 'sidsten', "the last one"].
  - Builds are announced: "melde, at han/hun 'bygger 9'" [announce "building 9"].
  - Same-rank builds: "melde 'mere syv'" [announce "more seven"].
  - Opponents must ask: "Hvis der ligger en 4 på bordet, og man lægger en 4 mere på og glemmer at fortælle, om det er 8 eller mere 4, er det modstanderens pligt at spørge, hvad det er, man bygger." [If you put a 4 on a 4 and forget to say whether it's 8 or "more 4", it is the opponent's duty to ask what you are building.] [S11]
- **Card values:** "Ruder 10 (Store kasino) har værdien 10 eller 16. Spar 2 (Lille kasino) har værdien 2 eller 15. Esser har værdien 1 eller 14. **Spar 5 rydder bordet.**" [♦10 (Big casino) is 10 or 16; ♠2 (Little casino) 2 or 15; aces 1 or 14; **the ♠5 clears the table**.] Also: "Hvis man smider spar 5 på bordet uden at rydde, bliver spar 5 automatisk til en almindelig 5." [If you throw the ♠5 down without clearing, it becomes an ordinary 5.] [S11]
  - History: the ♠5 rule was in the **2005 first version**: "Derudover kan spar 5 rydde bordet." [S13]. In 2021 an editor added "(Dette er en tilføjelse til de oprindelige regler)" [this is an addition to the original rules], and it was removed as "kildeløst og tvivlsomt" [unsourced and dubious] [S13].
  - A talk-page question from 2025: "må man rydde bordet med spar 5, hvis det er ens sidste kort i sidste runde så man både får point for rydningen og for sidsten?" [may you clear the table with the ♠5 if it is your last card in the last round, so you get points both for the clearing and for *sidsten*?] [S12]
- **Building:** "Dette kaldes at bygge dobbelt … bygge trippelt" [this is called building double … triple]. "Hvis man bygger noget, SKAL man tage et stik ind den efterfølgende tur, dog behøver det ikke være det, man lige har bygget." [If you build, you MUST take in a trick on the following turn, though not necessarily the one you just built.] "Man må ikke selv lave flere bygninger, før den forrige er samlet op" [you may not make more builds before the previous one is picked up]. "Der kan ikke bygges til et kort man IKKE selv har på hånden" [you cannot build to a card you don't hold] [S11].
- **Scoring:** "1 eller 2 point for flest spar; 1 eller 2 point for flest kort; 2 point for ruder 10; 1 point for spar 2; 1 point for hvert es; 1 point for hver gang man ryddede bordet (svupper); 1 point for at få det sidste stik, sidsten". Ties score nothing [S11].
  - History: in 2005 the values were "2 point for flest spar, 1 point for flest kort" [S13]. "1 eller 2 for flest spar" was added in 2010 ("Flest spar kan også give to points"). In 2024 an IP edit to "2 point for flest kort" was reverted [S13].
- **End:** "Der spilles traditionelt det antal runder, der skal til, før en spiller har fået 21 point … Hvis der er mere end en der får 21 point så er det den der er tættere på 21 der vinder." [Traditionally played until a player reaches 21 … if more than one reaches 21, **whoever is closer to 21 wins**.] This is an overshoot rule shared with a rare Finnish variant [S11].
- **Alternative rules:** "Kombinationsregelen kan udvides, så både sum og difference af kortværdier kan benyttes … yderligere udvides med både produkt og kvotient" [the combination rule can be extended to allow differences, and even products and quotients] [S11].
- **History of the edition:**
  - The 2005 original said "2 spillere spiller 2 runder, 3 spillere 3 og 4 spillere 4" [2 players play 2 rounds, 3 play 3, 4 play 4]. Its phrasing ("Denne udgave af Kasino…") closely matches the Swedish Wikibooks page, which describes a *computer game* (§2.12) [S13, S36].
  - Removed IP additions:
    - Court cards "har værdien 'knægt' og kan derfor ikke bruges til at bygge med" [have the value 'jack' and so cannot be used to build] (2018, removed as unsourced/"tvivlsomt").
    - A penalty: "spredes kortene i bygge bunken, og spilleren straffes med to point" [the build is scattered and the player penalised two points]. It was removed as a "regel, der ikke står i Politikens spilledjævel" [a rule not found in *Politikens Spilledjævel*, the Danish standard rule book]. *Politikens Spilledjævel was not consulted.*
  - In 2022 the whole rules section was deleted as "langt uencyklopædisk" [long, unencyclopedic] and restored by an IP the same year [S13].
- Talk page, 2007: a reader asks whether two builds can be made in one play (with 3,4,5,6 on the table and 7, 2, K in hand). Answer: "Nej, men Wikipedia er ikke en brevkasse" [No, but Wikipedia is not an advice column], pointing to spil.tv2.dk rules [S12].

### 2.5 German — *Casino (Kartenspiel)* (rev 256341743) [S14–S16]
- "'''Casino''' (im englischen auch '''Cassino''') ist ein Karten-Raubspiel, dessen Wurzeln ins England am Ende des 18. Jahrhunderts zurückreichen." [Casino (in English also Cassino) is a card "robbing game" whose roots reach back to England at the end of the 18th century.] [S14]
  - **History:** from 2006 to 2022 the lead said the roots went back "ins **Frankreich des 17. Jahrhunderts**" [to 17th-century France]. Bermicourt changed it on 2022-07-20: "kaum in Frankreich gespielt; Nach Franco Pratesi ist das Spiel ursprünglich aus England" [hardly played in France; according to Franco Pratesi the game is originally from England] [S16].
- **Values:** Aces 1 or 14 "Augen (Pips)"; J 11, Q 12, K 13; "die ♠2 wird *Kleines Casino* genannt und zählt zwei oder fünfzehn Augen, und die ♦10, *Großes Casino* genannt, zehn oder sechzehn Augen" [♠2 is called Little Casino and counts 2 or 15; ♦10, Great Casino, counts 10 or 16] [S14].
- **Deal (anomalous):** "Beim Spiel zu zweit erhält jeder Spieler zwölf Karten. Nehmen vier Personen am Spiel teil, so erhält jeder Spieler vier Karten" [with two players each gets **twelve** cards; with four, four cards each]. Three players get **seven** each. Four cards go to the table [S14]. (No other edition deals 12 or 7. Twelve is the full-deal arithmetic of 4+12+12=28, which is not 52. Treat this as an error or an unusual source.)
- **Actions:** *ablegen*, *paaren*, *kombinieren*, *bauen*. After a sweep (*geräumter Tisch*, "cleared table"), "muss der nächste Spieler – mangels anderer Möglichkeiten – eine Karte ablegen, man nennt dies *eine Karte für den Tisch bringen*" [the next player must lay a card; this is called "bringing a card for the table"] [S14].
- **Scoring:** "Kartenmehrheit, d. h. mit mindestens 27 eroberten Karten, erhält drei Punkte" [card majority, at least 27, scores 3]; Großes Casino 2; Kleines Casino 1; each Ace 1; "Mehrheit der Pique-Karten, d. h. für mindestens sieben" [spade majority, at least seven] 1; each *geräumter Tisch* 1. "Eine Partie ist gewonnen, sobald eine Partei 21 Punkte erreicht." [A game is won as soon as a side reaches 21.] With three players, "genügt … jeweils die relative Mehrheit" [a relative majority suffices] [S14].
- **Variants listed:** "*Royal Casino* mit englischen, nordamerikanischen, karibischen u. a. Varianten: *Dominican Casino*, *Hungarian Casino*; *African Casino* mit Varianten aus Swaziland, Lesotho und South Africa; *Nordic Casino* mit Varianten in Schweden und Finnland; Krypcasino auch aus Schweden". This mirrors pagat's structure [S14, S64].
- **Talk (2022):** "Bei Cassino gibt es einen Spielzug, der im Deutschen 'Kehraus' genannt wird, was ja auch genau die Übersetzung von Scopa wäre." [In Cassino there is a move called 'Kehraus' in German, which would be exactly the translation of Scopa.] [S15] The 1810 *Spielalmanach* confirms "Sweep, oder den Kehraus" [S83].
- **Spam history:** 2007 additions of online-casino links (888.com and others) were reverted. A 2020 addition mentioning "Kalifornien Cassino, African Cassino" was sourced partly to a slot-machine site, and the commercial link was removed [S16]. The title collision with gambling casinos attracts spam in several editions (also nb 2023 and sv 2023).

### 2.6 Finnish — *Kasino (korttipeli)* (rev 23498310) [S17–S19]
- **Infobox card order:** "10♦-2♠-A-K-Q-J-10-9-8-7-6-5-4-3-2 (kädessä) / K-Q-J-10-9-8-7-6-5-4-3-2-A (pöydässä)" [in hand / on the table]. This dual ranking is a compact way to describe hand values versus table values [S17 rendered].
- "Peliä voi pelata 2–4 henkilöä ja neljällä henkilöllä sitä voi pelata myös pareittain" [2–4 can play; four may also play in pairs]. Main source: Mikko Saari, Korttipeliopas (2010) [S17], **verified** [S67].
- **Values:** "♦10 (valttikortti *iso kasino*, jonka arvo on kädessä 16, mutta pöydässä normaalisti 10)" [trump card "big casino", value 16 in hand but normally 10 on the table]; "♠2 (*pieni kasino* … kädessä 15 … pöydässä 2)" ["small casino" 15 in hand, 2 on the table]; "Ässä (arvo kädessä 14, mutta pöydässä 1)" [Ace 14 in hand, 1 on the table]. Face cards 11/12/13 in Finnish play: "Suomalaisessa kasinossa kuvakorteilla on yleensä numeroarvot (11, 12 ja 13), mutta muualla usein ei" [in Finnish Kasino the face cards usually have number values 11, 12 and 13, but elsewhere often not] [S17]. Pagat adds the consequence: "Therefore an Ace cannot capture a single Ace" [S65].
- **Terms:**
  - *Nostaminen* (lifting/capturing) and *Pöytääminen* ("tabling", i.e. trailing) [S17].
  - Korttipeliopas uses "Etukäsi aloittaa" [forehand begins] and "summakortti" [sum card] [S67].
- **Sweep = mökki** ["cottage, hut"]: "Jos pelaaja nostaa omalla vuorollaan kaikki kortit pöydästä, hän saa mökin eli ylimääräisen pisteen." [If a player lifts all cards from the table on their turn, they get a *mökki*, i.e. an extra point.]
  - **Cancelling:** "jos pelaaja tyhjentää pöydän, kun kaikilla muilla pelaajilla on kullakin vähintään yksi mökki, lähtee heiltä yksi sellainen pois" [if a player clears the table when all others already have at least one mökki, they each lose one]. The verb used is **kaatua** ("to fall/topple"): "heiltä kaikilta kaatuu eli poistuu yhdet mökit" [one mökki 'falls', i.e. is removed, from each of them].
  - No mökki in the last deal.
  - **Marking:** "laittamalla yksi pinon korteista poikittain kuvapuoli ylöspäin (Pisteettömän kortin käyttö on suositeltavaa pistelaskun selkeyttämiseksi)" [placing one card of the pile *crosswise*, face up; using a non-scoring card is recommended to keep scoring clear] [S17].
- **Tactics:** "jos pöydässä on 3, 5, 8 ja 9, voi ottaa 8:lla 3:n ja 5:n ja jättää 8:n ottamatta estäen siten seuraavan pelaajan mahdollisen mökin" [with 3,5,8,9 on the table you can take 3+5 with an 8 and *leave* the 8, blocking the next player's possible mökki]. Also: you may play an 8 onto a table 8 without taking it, "esimerkiksi ruutu 10:tä tai kolmatta kahdeksikkoa varten" [e.g. saving them for the ♦10 or a third eight] [S17].
- **Scoring (10 base points):**
  - ♦10 2; ♠2 1; each Ace 1.
  - *Korttivoitto* (most cards) 1. *Patavoitto* (most spades) **2**.
  - Mökki 1 each.
  - **Ties carry over ("jää pakkaan" = "stays in the deck"):** a cards tie makes the next deal's cards point worth 2, then 3, and so on; a spades tie makes it 4, then 6, and so on [S17].
  - Korttipeliopas confirms the carry-over, but notes that in a three-player two-way spade tie the points are halved [S67].
  - The first version of the article (2006) already had this carry-over rule and called the game "pelataan paljon Suomessa" [played a lot in Finland] [S19].
- **Target:** "Ensimmäiseksi 16 pistettä tai enemmän kerännyt pelaaja on voittaja" [first to 16 or more wins]. Play either always to the end of the pack or stop as soon as someone hits 16 [S17].
- **Variants (named):**
  - *Laistokasino* ("shirking casino") = *misäärikasino* (misère casino): avoid points, and capturing is compulsory (*ottopakko*).
  - *Rakennuskasino* ("building casino"): building is allowed; mökki usually no points; cards 3 and spades 1; play to 21. A same-value pair of builds makes a *kaksoisrakennus* (double build) that "ei voi enää korottaa" [can no longer be raised].
  - *Pakkakasino* ("deck casino"): hands are refilled to 4 immediately.
  - Others: a point for the last capture; play to 21; an exact-16 variant where "17 pisteeseen päätyessä joutuu 15:een, 18 pisteeseen päätyessä 14:een" [ending on 17 sends you to 15, on 18 to 14]; fractional split of tied points (rare); mökki allowed in the last deal; "Mökkejä ei voi saada jos joku on kerännyt 10 pistettä tai sen yli" [no mökki once anyone has 10+ points] [S17].
- **Korttipeliopas extras (verified, not in Wikipedia):**
  - Building announcement: "sanoa 'rakennan ässälle'" [say "I build for the ace"], and "'rakennan yhdeksikölle'" ["I build for the nine"].
  - Same-number builds: "pitää sanoa selvästi rakentaako kolmoselle vai kuutoselle" [you must say clearly whether you are building for three or for six].
  - Reader comments mention **"pimeä kasino"** ["dark casino"], where the player always turns up the top card of their hand instead of choosing — "onnenpeli, johon perheen pienimmätkin voivat osallistua" [a luck game the youngest in the family can join]. A house rule allows only one mökki per hand. A 2016 commenter recommends the game for teaching children arithmetic.
  - Saari dismisses a casino-marketing site's Kasino rules as possibly "tehty generatiivisella tekoälyllä" [made with generative AI] [S67].
- **History claim:** "Kasino-korttipelin ensimmäiset merkinnät löytyvät 1700-luvun Englannista, ja pelin uskotaan olevan sukua italialaiselle Scopa-pelille … Suomessa kasino on ollut suosittu jo vuosikymmeniä" [the first records are from 18th-century England; it is believed related to Italian Scopa; Kasino has been popular in Finland for decades]. **Unsourced** [S17].
- **Related:** "*Piitti* on Pohjois-Suomessa suosittu perinteikäs peli, joka myös suuresti muistuttaa kasinoa" [Piitti is a traditional game popular in northern Finland that strongly resembles Kasino]. It is cited to a "Kilpa piitin säännöt" [competition Piitti rules] Facebook note of the Tornionlaakson tupenpelaajat, with rules approved 24.10.2000 [S17]. *That Facebook note was not accessible.*
- **Statistic/claim:** "Kasinossa korttien jakotapa mahdollistaa teoreettiset pelaajamäärät 2, 3, 4, 6, ja 12, ja niillä saa yhdestä pakasta aina 6, 4, 3, 2, ja 1 jakoa" [the dealing method allows player counts of 2, 3, 4, 6 and 12, giving 6, 4, 3, 2 and 1 deals per pack]. This follows from (52−4)/(4n) [S17].
- **History:**
  - 2025-08-15: a passage sourced to the gambling-marketing site kasinohai.com was removed ("ei tuollaista rakentamissääntöä voi olla, koska se on koko Rakennuskasinon pointti" [there can't be such a building rule, because that is the whole point of Rakennuskasino]) [S19, S18].
  - Talk (2014): "Itse – samoin kuin monet tuttavistani – ovat pelanneet 10 pistettä per jako -säännöillä, jolloin viimeisten oton tekijä ei saa yhtä lisäpistettä" [I and many acquaintances play with 10 points per deal, with no extra point for the last capture] [S18].

### 2.7 Hungarian — *Kaszinó (kártyajáték)* (rev 25838886) and the "Példa" subpage [S20–S22]
- "A **kaszinó** kétszemélyes kártyajáték" [a two-person card game]. "A játék közepesen nehéz, a siker nagyjából egyforma mértékben múlik a lapjáráson és a játékos szellemi teljesítményén. Rövid távú taktika megválasztására és – szokatlan módon – számolásra van hozzá szükség." [Medium difficulty; success depends roughly equally on the run of the cards and the player's mental performance. It needs short-term tactics and, unusually, arithmetic.] [S20]
- **History claim:** "A kaszinó a 20. század elején népszerű volt Magyarországon. Eredetéül egy középkori olasz kártyajáték, a scopa szolgált" [Kaszinó was popular in Hungary in the early 20th century. Its origin was a *medieval* Italian card game, scopa]. Unsourced, and it contradicts Pratesi [S20].
- **Values:** A=1, J=11, Q=12, K=13 [S20].
- **Structure:** "A parti általában 1-2 osztásból áll, a megnyeréséhez 11 pontot kell … összegyűjtenie" [a game is usually 1–2 deals; 11 points to win]. "Egy osztás 8 menetre oszlik" [a deal has 8 rounds]. Deal is **3 cards each**, then 4 to the table; the rest is the *talon* [S20].
- **Unique capture rule:** "A játékos teendője a kezében levő lapokból és az asztalon levő lapokból is kiválasztani egy-egy olyan kombinációt, amelyek pontértéke azonos, de legfeljebb 13." [The player chooses a combination from the cards *in hand* and one from the cards *on the table* with equal value, at most 13.] Example: "5+A=2+4, K=4+9, K=2+4+7" [S20]. The MEK rules confirm it: "Egyszerre 13-nál többet nem lehet … (kézben levő 3, 5 és 7 = az asztalon levő B és A). Csak egy cserét lehet csinálni." [No more than 13 at once … (3, 5 and 7 in hand = J and A on the table). Only one exchange can be made.] [S73]
- **Terms:**
  - A capture is a "**csere**" [exchange] or "**ütés**" [hit/trick].
  - The capturer moves the captured cards toward themselves "hogy az ellenfél összevethesse azokat" [so the opponent can check them]. Captured cards are kept in order "hogy az utolsó ütés helyessége is ellenőrizhető legyen" [so the correctness of the last capture can be checked].
  - No drawing after a capture [S20].
- **Sweep = tábla** ["board/table"]: "Ha egy játékos az ütés során az asztalon levő összes lapot felveszi, akkor mondjuk, hogy *táblát csinált* … (A tábla helyett a *söprés* szó is használt. Az olasz *scopa* magyarul seprű.)" [If a player picks up all the table cards, we say they "made a table" … (the word *söprés*, "sweeping", is also used; Italian *scopa* is "broom" in Hungarian.)]
  - A tábla scores 1, or **2 if made on the first turn after the deal**.
  - **Double move:** "Tábla után az ellenfél következik, neki egy lapot az asztalra kell tennie. Ezután kivételesen *ismét ő következik*" [after a tábla the opponent must put a card on the table; then, exceptionally, *it is their turn again*] [S20].
- **Draw:** "A talon tetejéről három új lapot kap az a játékos, aki az utolsó ütést csinálta" [the last capturer draws three new cards first]. "A leosztás utolsó, nyolcadik húzásakor a játékosok megkapott lapjaikat képpel felfelé az asztalra teszik. Az utolsó menet *nyitott lapokkal* folyik." [At the eighth and last draw, players put their cards face up; the last round is played *open*.] [S20]
- **Scoring:**
  - 3 for at least 27 cards; **2** for at least 7 spades.
  - 1 per ace; 2 for ♦10 ("*nagykaszinó*", big casino); 1 for ♠2 ("*kiskaszinó*", little casino).
  - 1 per tábla (2 on the first turn).
  - With 3 players the game has 5 rounds (4 cards in the last); with 4 players, 4 rounds [S20].
- **Spoken calls:**
  - „Amint a játékos már tudja, hogy elérte a 11 pontot, akkor ezt „*Ausz!*” vagy „*Kint vagyok!*” felkiáltással jelzi … Ha a játékos *nem jelzi* … és később a másik játékos jelenti be a saját eredményét, akkor *a másik játékos nyert*.” [As soon as a player knows they have reached 11, they signal it by exclaiming "Ausz!" (from German *aus*, "out") or "Kint vagyok!" ("I'm out!") … if they don't announce it and the other player later announces their own result, *the other player wins*.]
  - Winning outside the first deal earns only half the stake [S20].
  - Variant: „Ha egy játékos úgy véli, hogy az ellenfele helytelen kombinációval ütött … a „*Fals!*” szóval jelzi, hogy ellenőrzést kér … Ha a játékos valóban helytelen ütést csinált, az ellenfele **2** pontot kap … Ha az ütés mégis helyesnek bizonyul, az ütést csináló játékos **1** pontot kap.” [If a player thinks the opponent captured with a wrong combination, they say "Fals!" ("false!") to request a check. If the capture was indeed wrong, the opponent gets 2 points; if it proves correct, the capturer gets 1 point.]
  - Variant: after a tábla the opponent may say „*passz*” for the forced second move [S20].
  - Other variants: max two hand cards per capture; cards 2 and spades 1; play for a fixed number of deals. A rule that the opponent of the last capturer leads after the draw "a játékosokat passzivitásra ösztönzi" [encourages passivity] [S20].
- **The "Példa" subpage** [S21] is a fully annotated 8-round sample game in a custom text notation. In each line, `=` marks a capture, `>`/`>>` a card thrown to the table, `T` a tábla, `P`/"passz" a pass, `!!` a strong move, `?`/`?!` a dubious move, and `(…)` an alternative line.
  - Commentary examples: Bal ("Left") "a kisebb számértékű lapot választotta, mert ha az asztalra egy 3-as kerül mellé, a 9-esével felveheti" [chose the lower card, because if a 3 lands next to it she can pick them up with her 9]. In round 7, Jobb ("Right") makes "egy előkészítő lépéssel" [a preparatory move].
  - Final tally: Left 12 – Right 6 (cards 27/25 → 3 points; spades 8/5 → 2; aces 3/1; ♠2; ♦10; táblas 2/5). "ha még egyetlen lap Jobbhoz kerül, Bal összeredményéből elveszett volna 3 pont" [had just one more card gone to Right, Left would have lost 3 points].
  - *Useful as a ready-made replay format and test fixture.*
- **History:** a screenshot of a computer Kaszinó game was removed in 2021 as copyrighted ("jogvédett képernyőkép ki, ez a szócikk a játékról szól, nem egy számítógépes játékról" [copyrighted screenshot removed; this article is about the game, not a computer game]) [S22].
- **Sources the article cites:** *Kártyások könyve* (Sport, Budapest, 1984, "dr. Berend Mihály: Kaszinó") — not accessed. *Nagy játékkönyv* (1996) — not accessed. MEK "Kártyajátékok szabályai" — **accessed** [S73]. Játékgyűjtemény (archived 2009) — **accessed**, and it repeats "AUSZ vagy KINT VAGYOK bemondással jelezzük" [we signal it by announcing AUSZ or KINT VAGYOK] [S74].

### 2.8 Italian — *Cassino (gioco)* (rev 152703178; created 2026-09-01) [S23]
- "**Cassino**, noto anche come **Casino**, è un gioco di carte inglese" [an English card game]. 2–4 players, or two pairs [S23].
- **Values:** "Caso particolare del K, Q e J, essi non hanno un valore numerico" [K, Q and J have no numeric value]. "Il K, Q e J possono essere presi solo con lo stesso 'valore' e uno alla volta." [K, Q, J can only be taken by the same "value", one at a time.] [S23]
- **Dealer selection:** lowest card deals (ace lowest). Deal in twos: opponent, table, self [S23].
- **Actions** (with English terms kept): *Pairing*, *Combining*, *Trailing*, *Building* [S23].
- **Calls:** "costruisco un sei" ["I build a six"], "costruisco un otto", "costruisco un sette", "costruisco un dieci" [S23].
  - Simple builds can be "**rubare**" [stolen] or "**estendere**" [extended].
  - **Multiple builds** "non possono essere aumentate di valore".
  - A builder "non può né scartare una carta né può nuovamente costruire da zero" [can neither trail nor start a new build from scratch] on their next turn [S23].
- **Sweep:** "si realizza uno 'sweep', che vale un punto … Per contrassegnare uno sweep effettuato, il giocatore pone a faccia in su la carta che gli ha permesso di fare lo sweep" [a 'sweep' is worth one point … to mark it, the player puts face up the card that made the sweep] [S23].
- **Scoring:** "Sweep" (Scope) 1; "Cards" 3; "Great Cassino" 2; "Little Cassino" 1; "Aces" 1; "Spades" 1. Game to "undici o ventuno" [11 or 21]; a tie plays another round [S23].
- **Variants:**
  - Royal (J11 Q12 K13, A 1/14).
  - **Spade Cassino "potendo ottenere così, 25 punti in una sola partita … vince chi prima o supera 61"** [up to 25 points per game; first to 61]. *25 contradicts Foster's verified 24 [S78].*
  - Draw ("Royal Draw Cassino").
  - **"Italian Cassino", also called Diamond Cassino**, which adds a point for the ♦7 and uses 40 cards and 3-card hands, "rendendo simile a Scopa" [making it similar to Scopa] [S23].
- **Reference quality problems (checked):**
  - The it article cites **gambiter.com**, which is a copy of an old English Wikipedia text **vandalised** to read "Cassino … is a Madeirense fishing card game … via Luís Ferreira, an immigrant from Fiscal … mostly played by two with a Bicycle deck".   - gamerules.com and playingcarddecks.com (both cited) do support the 2-at-a-time deal, the "building six" style announcements, and the dealer announcing "last" [S68, S69].
  - Britannica (David Parlett) supports: 2–4 players, "best played with two"; "1 point for each sweep, ace, and little casino, 2 points for big casino, 1 point for taking the most spades, and 3 points for taking the most cards"; "Game is 11 or 21 points" [S66].

### 2.9 Japanese — *カシノ* (rev 106408641) [S24–S25]
- "英語圏で行われるトランプゲームのひとつ。日本の花札の花合わせやこいこいに似た特徴を持つ。" [One of the card games played in the English-speaking world. It has features similar to the Japanese hanafuda games Hana-awase and Koi-koi.] [S24]
- **Player-count arithmetic:** "ジョーカーを除いた一組52枚のうち、4枚を並べて場札としたときに残りが48枚となり、8枚（2人…）、12枚（3人…）、16枚（4人…）と配った際に余りが出ない（つまり、48は8、12、16の公倍数となる）" [of 52 cards, laying 4 as table cards leaves 48; dealing 8 (2 players), 12 (3) or 16 (4) per round leaves no remainder, i.e. 48 is a common multiple of 8, 12 and 16] [S24].
- **History, current:** "カシノのルールは…スコパというゲームによく似ている。このため、イタリアから伝わったものと考えられるが、そのことを示す積極的な証拠があるわけではない。カシノに関する最古の文献は18世紀末のロンドンのものである" [The rules closely resemble Scopa, so it is thought to have come from Italy, but there is no positive evidence. The oldest document is from late-18th-century London] (cites pagat). "「Cassino」という綴りはイタリアのモンテ・カッシーノを思わせるが、古い時代には「Casino」と綴られるのが普通だった" [the spelling "Cassino" evokes Italy's Monte Cassino, but in old times "Casino" was the usual spelling]. "賭博場を意味するカジノ（casino）が英語の文献に現れるのは19世紀以降であり、カードゲームのカシノより新しい" [casino meaning a gambling house appears in English only from the 19th century, later than the card game] [S24].
- **History, removed in 2013:** "15世紀ごろのフランスでギャンブルとして行われたのが発祥である。カジノでこのゲームが行われていたため、このゲームが「カシノ」と呼ばれるようになった。（日本ではギャンブル施設をカジノと発音するが、フランスではカシノに近い発音になる。）" [It originated as gambling in France around the 15th century. Because it was played in casinos it came to be called 'kashino'. (In Japan the gambling facility is pronounced 'kajino', but in France the pronunciation is close to 'kashino'.)] It was rewritten on 2013-07-18 "pagat.com に従って" [following pagat.com] [S25].
- **Terminology (an inverted mapping, worth noting for localisation):**
  - *テイク* (take) = pairing.
  - **ビルド (build) = capturing by *sum***: "手札のうち1枚と、合計が同じ数字になる2枚以上の数札が場札にあれば…取ることができる" [if two or more number cards on the table total the same as a hand card, you can take them].
  - What English calls building or calling is **付け札 (*tsukefuda*, "attached card")**.
  - So Japanese "build" ≠ English "build".
  - "絵札は数字にはならない（そのため、ビルドで取ることができない）" [face cards are not numbers, so cannot be taken by "build"].
  - "同位札" [same-rank card] [S24].
- **Tsukefuda rules:** a same-rank tsukefuda can be taken only by take, not by "build" (e.g. 2+2 cannot be taken with a 4). It cannot be combined with other table cards, and "付け札を1枚ずつ切り離して…取ることもできない" [the attached cards cannot be separated] [S24].
- **Deal:** "Aは2枚ずつ2回に分けてカードを配り手札とする" [the dealer deals 2 cards twice]; then 4 table cards. A sweep is marked with one card face up. Game to 21 [S24].
- **Scoring:** カード点 3, スペード 1, ビッグ・カジノ 2 (♦10), リトル・カジノ 1 (♠2), エース 1, スイープ 1 [S24].
  - A hidden editor comment (2012): a rule counting "taking all but one card" as a sweep was removed, because "一枚だけ残して取った場合も「スイープ」として扱っているルールを発見できなかった" [could not find any rules treating taking all but one card as a sweep] [S25].
- Sources listed (not accessed): *プレイ・ザ・トランプ* (JTB, 1986) and *トランプ入門* (Shogakukan, 1983) [S24].

### 2.10 Norwegian Bokmål — *Kasino (kortspill)* (rev 25246971) [S26–S28]
- Infobox: 2–4 players, ages 8+, difficulty "Nokså lett" [fairly easy]. Tagged "Helt uten kilder" [entirely without sources] [S26].
- **Origin:** "Spillet stammer fra Italia, og er sannsynligvis en avart av det populære italienske kortspillet Scopa." [The game comes from Italy and is probably a variant of Scopa.] **Unsourced**, and it contradicts Pratesi [S26].
- **Deal:** 4 each and 4 face up. "Hvis et av kortene i bordet er et poengkort, legges de tilbake i kortstokken, og erstattes av et nytt kort." [If a table card is a point card, it goes back into the deck and is replaced.] [S26]
- **Values:** "En spar to har verdien 2 på bordet, men 15 på hånden. Ruter 10 har verdien 10 på bordet, men 16 på hånden. Et ess har verdien 1 på bordet, men 14 på hånden." [♠2: 2 on table, 15 in hand; ♦10: 10 / 16; ace: 1 / 14.] [S26]
- **Sweep = tabbe:** "En spiller som tar inn alle kortene på bordet i ett stikk får et ekstrapoeng, en «tabbe»." [A player who takes all the table cards in one trick gets an extra point, a "tabbe".] Scoring: most cards 1, most spades 1, last trick 1, each ace 1, ♠2 1, ♦10 2, each tabbe 1 [S26].
- **Building:** "«doble» et bygg" ["double" a build]. Example: with 8 and queen in hand, put the 8 on a table 4 [S26].
- **Strategy section:** "Man vil for enhver pris unngå å gi fra seg poengkort … Kort av fargen spar gir høyere sannsynlighet for poeng … vil man kanskje velge å spolere muligheten hans ved å unngå at det ligger en samlet verdi av 14, 15 eller 16 på bordet." [You want at all costs to avoid giving away point cards … spades give a higher chance of points … you may spoil the opponent's chance by avoiding a table total of 14, 15 or 16.] [S26]
- **Variants:**
  - "Smyg eller *krypkasino* – Variant hvor man skal unngå å ta poeng. Når en legger ut et kort må en ta inn kort hvis tallkombinasjonene av kortene tilsier det" [Smyg ("sneak") or krypkasino ("crawl-casino") – avoid points; capturing is compulsory].
  - Multiple builds at once; building on your own build; **5 players**: only 2 table cards and a "half" round.
  - "**Lokale navn finnes også, for eksempel kan spillet kalles tabbe og siste runde kalles «døgga».**" [Local names exist too: the game can be called *tabbe*, and the last round *døgga*.] [S26]
- **Talk page (2022, user Bbl):**
  - "med unntak av en spiller har samtlige regnet to poeng for sparen og ett for kortene" [with one exception, everyone I've played with scored 2 for spades and 1 for cards]. "en god spiller vil telle antall spar hver spiller til enhver tid har tatt, og i gitte tilfeller f.eks velge å ikke ta et ess for å sikre seg sin syvende spar" [a good player counts each player's spades at all times and may decline an ace to secure their seventh spade].
  - "**Tabbe** er et midlertidig poeng … kan motspilleren annulere dette poenget senere ved å selv renske bordet … Tabben markeres ved å vende et kort (som ikke er poengkort eller spar) med bildesiden opp i stokken." [A tabbe is a *provisional* point … the opponent can cancel it by clearing the table themselves … it is marked by turning a card (not a point card or a spade) face up in the pile.]
  - Money play "først til 16 poeng" [first to 16]: "kan en følge med på egne sikre poeng og avslutte spillet i det øyeblikket en drar inn sitt 16. sikre poeng … Hvis en avslutter spillet, men har har telt feil … så vinner motstanderen automatisk." [you track your *safe* points and end the game the moment you take your 16th safe point … if you end it having miscounted, the opponent wins automatically.]
  - "**Slutten/sisten** (siste stikk) … Hvis den som gir *ikke* melder fra om at det er siste delerunde *før* forhånda legger sitt første kort i bordet, teller poenget kun dersom forhånda får slutten" [if the dealer does *not* announce the last deal before forehand plays, the last-trick point counts only if forehand gets it; the reverse applies in smyg/kryp] [S27].
- **Removed text** (2013–2020 version, rev 19599819) [S28]:
  - "Siste runde annonseres av utdeler som «sisten»" [the dealer announces the last round as "sisten"].
  - Terms section "Begreper": *Ta*, *Bygg*, *Å Doble*, *Tabbe*. "En spiller kan også «stjele» en annen spillers bygg" [a player can "steal" another's build]. "Det er dog ikke lov å legge ut et kort uten å ta noe sålenge man har et bygg" [you may not trail while you have a build].
  - Scoring then: "2 poeng for flest spar; 2 poeng for ruterti (store kasino); 1 poeng for sparto (lille kasino) … 1 poeng for siste stikk. Totalt 11 poeng". "Får alle spillerne en tabbe, stryker hver spiller tabben." [If all players have a tabbe, each crosses it off.]
  - Tactics: "har ikke sparto blitt delt før siste runde, og en selv ikke har den, vet en at motspilleren må ha den på hånden, og en ønsker følgelig ikke at det ligger kort med summen av 15 på bordet" [if the ♠2 hasn't appeared before the last round and you don't have it, the opponent must hold it, so avoid leaving a table sum of 15].
  - An old "Generelt" box gave "Oppsett: 30−60 sekunder. Spill: 5−10 minutter." [setup 30–60 s; play 5–10 min].
- **Removed IP edits** [S28]:
  - "16 poeng for tabbe på ruterti; 8 poeng for tabbe på sparto" [16 points for a tabbe made with the ♦10; 8 for a tabbe made with the ♠2] (2010, reverted).
  - "3 poeng for flest spar" (2016, reverted).
  - "Det er lov til å bygge flere bygg samtidig. Sier man noe annet er man en DUST og dårlig taper!!!" [It's allowed to have several builds at once. Anyone who says otherwise is an IDIOT and a sore loser!!!] (2011, reverted). *Table talk evidence that the multiple-build rule is contested.*
  - The title was moved from *Casino* to *Kasino* in 2010: "Offisiell skrivemåte iflg Språkrådet" [official spelling according to the Language Council] [S28].

  - Variants: "**kryp-mulle**" and "**rövarmulle**" ["robber-mulle": you may steal the opponent's pile if you have a card equal to its top card].
  - Scores: "Förlust med mer än 100 poäng ger plats i '**hundraklubben**'. Detta kallas ibland populärt för '**senap**'.   - **Stelen/Stehlen** variant: capture piles are kept face up, and a matching hand card steals an opponent's whole pile.
  - Pitched for children: "ideaal spel om kinderen te leren omgaan met kaarten en te leren tellen" [ideal for teaching children to handle cards and to count]; "Zum Lernen der Spielregeln … kann anfangs mit offenen Karten gespielt werden" [for learning, play with open hands at first].

## 6. Scorekeepers, markers and physical or digital affordances on Wikipedia

- **Marking sweeps:**
  - "Captured cards are placed in a trick pile, face up" (English, Long) [S1].
  - Italian: the sweeping card is turned face up [S23]. Japanese: one card face up [S24].
  - Danish: "vender det øverste kort i sin stik bunke" [turns the top card of the trick pile] [S11].
  - Finnish: one card "poikittain kuvapuoli ylöspäin" [crosswise, face up], ideally a non-point card [S17].
  - Swedish (pagat): the capturing card is placed face up "rotated so that the card remains visible" [S65].
  - Norwegian talk: turn a non-point, non-spade card [S27].
  - Mulle: a tabbe is marked by a face-*down* card, because the piles are face up [S37].
  - Wippen: the card is turned over or crosswise [S59].
  - Zwicker (Grupp): turn a card or place it "at right angles" [S40].
  - Bastra: "the bastra card face up, sticking out of the player's pile" [S43].
  - Scopa: the face-up sweep card is also there "to taunt" [S38].
- **Scoring media:**
  - Foster (1897): "the score may be kept with counters, on a sheet of paper, or on a cribbage board". Twenty-one Point Cassino is "usually marked with counters, or pegged on a cribbage board". Spade Cassino is pegged as it happens, "once round the board and into the game hole" [S78].
  - The 1810 Spielalmanach prints a circle diagram for marking points [S83].
  - Cuarenta: "two kinds of chips, the two-point tantos (points) and the ten-point perros (dogs). When a standard 52 card pack is used, the 8s, 9s and 10s can be used to keep the score" [S44].
  - Skwitz: each player has "a dish for their own chips … and a larger dish for the pot" [S46]. Culbas and Papillon use *fiches* and *jetons* and a basket (*corbillon*) [S53, S54].
- **Checkability:**
  - Hungarian players move captured cards toward themselves so the opponent can check them, and stack piles in order so the last capture can be audited, supporting the "Fals!" challenge [S20].
  - Hoyle 1796 and the 1868 American Hoyle: only the last trick may be looked at [S82, S79].
- **Digital:**
  - Swedish Wikibooks describes a mouse-driven Kasino client: left-click selects capture cards, right-click selects the build card; "Bygg" and "Bygg mera" buttons; a `*x*` suffix on a multiple build's value; an "Avancerade rummet" [Advanced room] for 4-player partnership [S36].
  - hu Wikipedia once showed a screenshot of a computer Kaszinó (removed 2021) [S22].
  - Pagat lists the SpiteNET: Cassino program, Thanos Card Games, a Games4All Android app and a PSellos WebApp [S64].
  - en Scopa mentions a Scopa minigame in the Nancy Drew game *The Phantom of Venice* [S38].
- **Notation:** the hu "Példa" subpage is a full text notation for recording and annotating a game [S21].
- **Physical table-layout tips:** "Many players deal the initial table cards in a 2x2 square" (Scopa) [S38]. Talk:Cassino notes that real tables "very, very rarely" hold more than about 8 cards, and that partial overlap of cards signals a build [S2].

## 7. Strategy content found on Wikipedia and in verified cited sources

- **Hoyle 1796:**
  - "The principal objects are to remember how many Tens, Aces, and Deuces have been played, and to clear the Hand of Court Cards, which are only of Service in pairing or in gaining the Sweep".
  - "In making Pairs, a Preference should always be given to Spades".
  - "When three Aces are out, take the first Opportunity to play the fourth … but when there is another Ace remaining, it is better even to play the little Cassino than to risk the Ace".
  - "When the Deuces are out, an eight is an eligible Lead; and when the Trays, a Seven".
  - "At the Commencement of a Game combine all the Cards possible, for that is more difficult than pairing" [S82].
- **Foster 1897:**
  - "The principal thing in Cassino is to remember what has been played".
  - "Take in the adversary's build in preference to your own, if you can, and build on his build at every opportunity".
  - "If Big Cassino is still to come, avoid trailing cards that will make a Ten".
  - "Go for 'cards' in preference to everything else".
  - "It is considered bad policy to take in three court cards, as it stops all sweeps when the fourth appears".
  - On trailing: "play the smaller cards, except Aces and Little Cassino" [S78].
- **1810 Spielalmanach:** the dealer keeps back a court card to secure the Kehraus. With Great Casino in hand (the other three tens out), trail a card that combines with the table to 10 *or 16*, hoping to collect it with the Great Casino [S83].
- **Removed en text:** building protects cards, and its value falls as players increase (2p vs 4p). The rule variant governing builds changes the builder's advantage [S5].
- **nb:** count spades; deny table sums of 14/15/16; deduce who holds the ♠2 if it hasn't appeared by the last round [S26, S28]. The talk page suggests forgoing an ace to secure the seventh spade [S27].
- **fi:** leave a capturable card on the table to block the next player's mökki; leave pairs for the ♦10 or a third card [S17].
- **Pagat Nordic** (cited by de/ja): hold a non-spade 2 or a non-diamond 10 to catch lillan or storan; trail ranks already played; as an alternative, play an Ace as 14 [S65].
- **Scopa:** "it is a common tactic … to capture aces and sixes whenever possible" (primiera) [S38].
- **hu Példa:** throw away the lower card so a 9 can later collect it with a 3; make "preparatory" moves late in the hand; in the open last round, sacrificing a card can be tactical [S21].

## 9. Related English articles: key facts relevant to the Cassino family

- **Scopa** [S38]:
  - "one of the three major national card games in Italy". 40 cards, 3 each, 4 to the table.
  - Must capture a single matching card over a sum: "the player is forced to capture only the single card".
  - "it is not legal to place on the table a card that has the ability to capture".
  - A deal with three or four kings on the table is invalid.
  - Points: cards, coins, settebello, primiera (7=21, 6=18, A=16, 5=15, 4=14, 3=13, 2=12, face cards 10), scopas. Game 11.
  - Variants: Scopone (9 or 10 cards each), Scopa d'Assi (an ace takes all), Napola, Re Bello, Scopa a Quindici, Cirulla (Ligurian terms *barsega*, *boña da trei/dexe*, *poncin*/*matta* wild 7♥, *a bassa*/*la piccola*, *l'erta*/*la grande*, *sgobba*), Libyan Scopa.
  - History: allegedly "originated from two Spanish games called Primero and Scarabucion … pirates and fishermen would stop to gamble" (sourced to a gambling operator, snai.it).
  - Talk: "Scopa at the last round … I can't find any rule where this is specified" (2020) [S39].
- **Zwickern / Zwicker** [S40, S41]:
  - "a simpler and jollier version of Cassino" (Parlett). Hans Fallada called it "a rather cunning farmer's game from Holstein".
  - First recorded 1918 (Fritz Lau); rules 1930 (Hülsemann). Played "for a glass of beer or a Grog".
  - Up to 6 Jokers. Special 58-card packs (ASS, NSV) were made from the 1950s until about 2020.
  - Classic values: K 14, Q 13, J 12, A 11. Scoring: ♦10 10 points, ♦7 1, ♠7 1, each ace 2, Zwick 3, most cards 1.
  - Village variants (Dithmarschen, Grossenwiehe) have jokers with handwritten values (30/25/22/20/17/15).
  - de says Zwicker descends from Cassino, shown by English terms such as *Lurch* and *Sweep* in the early German sources [S41].
- **Skwitz** (Austria, 1852/1866) [S46]: said to be of English origin, "possibly in a game called Quits". Matadors are ♠J, ♠10 and ♠2. Played for chips. Calls: "Skwitz!", "Aus!"; "matsch".
- **Tablanette** [S45]:
  - Origin "supposed" Russian (Phillips & Westall 1939) or "the Orient" (Ulmann 1890).
  - "Table nette means 'clean table'", first used in connection with cards in the 1810 Spielalmanach as a sweep in Cassino (consistent with my reading of the 1810 text [S83]).
  - Rules: 6 cards each; K14, Q13, A 1/11; a Jack takes all; "Tablanette!" scores the card values; game 251.
  - Serbian Tablić / Romanian Tabinet: game 101; a sweep (*tabla*) is 1 point.
- **Pasur** (Iran) [S42]:
  - Sum to 11; a Jack takes all number cards. "Sur" is worth 5 (or 10).
  - Scoring: most clubs 7; ♦10 3; ♣2 2; aces and jacks 1. Game 62.
  - The name is said to come from Russian. Variant "Pasur Ru Baaz" [Open Pasur].
  - Talk: a removed rule that "a player that already has a total score of 50 or above may NOT score any more 'surs'".
- **Bastra / Basra** [S43]: popular in Cyprus, Egypt and Lebanon; 10-point basra; a Jack takes all. Regional variants: Egyptian (most cards 30), Yemeni, Ethiopian ("komai" = ♦7), Lebanese/Ashush, Palestinian (basra = 2× the card value), Syrian (Jack-on-Jack = 25), Piro (Uzbekistan). Talk: a Turkish-origin dispute and a "Bastra vs Basra?" title dispute.
- **Cuarenta** (Ecuador) [S44]: 40 cards; *caída* (capturing the card just played), *limpia* (sweep), *ronda*, chip scoring, official tournaments. Talk: Cuenca vs Quito rules.
- **Laugh and lie down** [S49]: "the earliest example of a European game of the fishing family", recorded 1522; rules by Willughby c.1665.
- **Culbas** (France, from 1658) and **Papillon** (1730; "a favourite game of the French king, Louis XVI") are early French fishing games [S53, S54].
- **Cicera** (Brescia): 52-card Bresciane deck, 12 cards each, rich dialect terminology [S52].
- **Escoba del 15 / Escova / Escopa**: capture to a sum of 15; Brazilian Escopa is a "game of the botequims", played "for beer, cigarettes, guaranás" [S50, S51].
- **Byggkasino** and **Mulle** (en stubs) mirror the sv content. Mulle is "a common game in Swedish prisons" [S47, S48].
